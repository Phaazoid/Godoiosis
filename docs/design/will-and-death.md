## The 2026-06-24 reframe — Will gates *limbs*, not *life*

The old ladder made Will a **life buffer**: you spent Will to convert a fatal hit into a down, and *running out of Will meant death*. The grill flipped it:

- **Will is a *limb / integrity* buffer.** A sub-overkill fatal hit **always** downs you (Will or not). Will only decides whether you go down **intact or maimed**.
- **Will never directly kills.** Death comes only from overkill, a failed rescue, or Crisis.

Why this is better: it removes the harsh "low Will → you just die" cliff (nobody dies from depletion — they get maimed and need rescue), it makes maim the *earned cost of fighting depleted* rather than a narrow edge rung (the dev's original worry), and — conveniently — it's **closer to the existing stub** (which already downs-unless-overkill regardless of Will) than the old design was. Determinism is preserved throughout (Law #1): no down-roll, no maim-roll; the down is unconditional and the maim is a threshold.

## The resource

- **Per-unit limb/integrity buffer.** Has a max and a current; spent, *not* regenerated in-battle. Own display bar under HP, different colour.
- **Max Will = the innate WIL stat** ([stats.md](stats.md)), **capped at `MAX_WILL = 20`** (`min(WIL, 20)`) — an identity number, set per-unit (incl. via the dev editor), shifting only via the authored/elective drift band. Default WIL ≈ 5, so bump it for a deep pool. ~4 downs' worth at WIL 20 / cost 5. (Shown on its own HP/WIL line, not as a stat row.)
- **Persists between missions** ([#8](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/8), on `UnitInstance`). Burnout is a campaign-level state paired with between-battle recovery (the Three Houses "motivation" feel).
- **It gates limbs, never life.** (The whole reframe.)

## The deterministic stakes ladder (Law #1)

A would-be-fatal hit, **sub-overkill**:

1. **Always DOWNED**, regardless of Will. The down **spends a flat cost `N`**.
2. **Can't afford `N` (Will < N) → down anyway, but MAIMED** — a limb is lost, Will floors at 0. Decided **at down-time** (so by the time you choose whether to revive, the limb is already gone or not). Maim is the *earned cost of fighting depleted*, not a separate rung.
3. **Overkill** (overshoot beyond remaining life exceeds the ceiling) → **dead outright** — now the *only* instant damage-death, so it carries more tuning weight than before.
4. **Downed + not rescued before the 3-turn countdown → dead.**

**Flat cost (not overshoot-scaled)** is deliberate: it keeps maim *depletion*-driven and legible (a unit is worth ~`Will / N` downs, and you can feel the cliff coming), and the overkill ceiling already supplies the "big hits are scary" drama — no need to bake it into the cost twice. (Overshoot-scaled cost is parked as a later knob.)

### Crisis Mode (an equipped ability — the one home of permadeath)

**REDESIGNED AND BUILT 2026-08-09 ([#158](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/158)), out of the [#124](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/124)/[#155](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/155) discussion that repealed the BREAK doctrine ([resolution-pipeline.md](resolution-pipeline.md) R9).** Crisis left the will-system core and became an **equipped ability** (`Abilities.Id.CRISIS`, kind = Reaction — the [jobs.md](jobs.md) chassis, whose "never a mid-pass prompt" rule it now obeys instead of excepting). First source: the **Berserker job** (`Resources/Jobs/Berserker.tres`, a pure ability carrier — nudges are open content); any kit source works (innate/jobs/gear).

The gambit as it stands — still **no death-save but a gambit for power**, accessible only by flirting with death:

- **Armed, never offered.** While the ability is held and Will is FULL (`LethalityRules.CRISIS_WILL_GATE` — the Will-gate mechanic mirrored from the down-cost, at the highest price), a would-be-down **ALWAYS** becomes Crisis, for any faction. Equipping the source IS the acceptance, made at loadout; execution asks nothing. Opting out = unequipping the source.
- **On firing:** the unit **stands straight back up** at `Abilities.CRISIS_REVIVE_HP` — it never transits DOWNED, emits no `went_downed`, queues no ejection (`Unit.take_damage` carries the CRISIS rung out directly via `enter_crisis()`, the same computation the resolver threads, so preview and execution are one thing). **Will → 0 and locked**, surge (+`CRISIS_SURGE` to STR/DEX/PER for `CRISIS_SURGE_TURNS`) primed for its next turn start.
- **For the rest of that battle there is no safety net** — no downs, no maims; **a would-be-down = permanent death.** You traded your net for the whole fight when you took the job.
- **The balance lever is authored WIL**, unchanged: early enemies don't carry the Will to hit the gate; later, the player scouts dangerous enemies by visible Will — and now also by their kit.

## Generation & recovery

**In-fight: depletion-only.** Within a fight Will mostly moves one direction — *down*. That is the dread the system runs on ("watch Will drain toward the cliff"). The grill's key catch: **every in-battle generator fights that cliff** — a passive tick is a boring timer that trivializes downs; kills/combos snowball and feel gamey ("why does a kill restore your nerve to *not die*?"). So Will is not freely generated in combat. Two sanctioned exceptions:

- **Rally** — a **third main action** (beside Attack and Rescue; via `BaseAction.is_main_action()`). **As built (2026-06-25, simplified per dev call): a *regular* main action** — Move-then-Rally is allowed (no hunker-in-place), restoring a **flat diminishing amount** (`RALLY_BASE = 6`, then −`RALLY_FALLOFF = 2` per use that battle, dropped once it would give < 1), clamped to max. `rally_count` is battle-scoped (resets each mission, lives on the transient `Unit`). *Fuller vision (deferred):* consume the whole turn in place and restore only toward a **partial cap** (never full) so a safe pocket can't be milked — for now the diminishing amount + the max clamp do that job. Lean on **scenario pressure** to punish pure turtling (level-design). *(Optional later: gate Rally to units near their leader — the old "leader inspiration" idea's home.)*
- The other in-fight relief valve is simply **rescue** — you recover *units* (drag the body back, fragile at 1 HP and low Will), not *nerve*.

> **Rescue gained a second job with [#116](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/116)

> the unit standing at 1 HP in a lake it could never have walked into — *"we need the rescue function

> the rescuer to bring the drowning unit onto"* (dev, same day). So `RulesService.rescue_landing`

## The limb-slot model — what a maim does (grilled 2026-07-04, BUILT 2026-07-15 — #56)

**Limbs are equipment slots, and the natural limb is the default gear.** Designed at the 2026-07-04 co-dev grill; built in code 2026-07-15:

- **Each arm slot** holds a natural arm (**arm STR = the unit's innate STR**), nothing (0), or a **prosthetic** (its own built-in STR + special effects). **Effective STR = the mean of the two arm slots, rounded UP** — a 7-STR unit maimed reads 4; a STR-10 prosthetic on that unit reads 9. Prosthetics can exceed the natural ceiling — the mechanist augmentation fantasy, landing exactly where [progression.md](progression.md) wants it.
- **Legs are fully symmetric — with DEX** (REVISED 2026-07-05, superseding the 2026-07-04 legs→MOV call: there is no SPD stat — DEX owns the speed role). Natural leg = innate DEX; effective DEX = mean of the two leg slots, rounded up. One rule, four slots: *arms carry your strength, legs carry your quickness.* Prosthetic legs bring their own DEX and can exceed baseline (pinned content ideas: rocket jumps, knee cannon). **MOV is not slot-averaged** — it's a visible tactics readout, **derivation BUILT 2026-07-15** ([jobs.md](jobs.md) → MOV): a maimed leg reaches MOV *through* DEX (the band), **and** through the direct leg throttle below — both effects stack.
- **Maim taxes aura (audit A3, ratified 2026-07-05): −1 aura point per lost limb**, off the unit's highest pool (ties → element enum order, a placeholder — the real tiebreak selector, possibly primary affinity, is open design work, [#77](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/77)) — restored by natural regrowth, kept lost by a prosthetic. Alchemists *really* don't want to be maimed; the chrome-vs-flesh trade is real ([alchemy-kit.md](alchemy-kit.md) → *Aura data model*).
- **Prosthetics pull double duty (audit A2, resolved 2026-07-05; weapon face BUILT 2026-07-17 — #59 item 6).** A prosthetic limb is both a working limb (its built-in stat feeds the slot average, so it wields ordinary weapons like flesh does) and — for weapon-model prosthetics — an ordinary `WeaponInstance` a unit equips/unequips freely like any weapon (no attack-geometry special-casing needed, contra this sections original framing); the only lock is that it **can't be tossed out of inventory** while installed (uninstalling is a between-mission action, not built). As a weapon it scales off **its own built-in STR, never the unit's** (mixed blends with the unit's other stats are allowed). Weapon face: [weapons.md](weapons.md) → Prosthetic family.
- **Verb locks stack on top** (provisional set): any missing arm → **two-handed/heavy patterns locked** + **rescue-carry locked**; off-hand gear interactions TBD with the equipment pass; *counter-gating on the two-handed lock is deferred to the two-handed weapon content pass (dev, 2026-07-15 — no weapon carries the flag yet, so the lock has no counter surface to police)*. Legs carry no extra verb **lock**, but they DO carry a direct MOV throttle *(dev ruling 2026-07-14 — supersedes the earlier "the DEX averaging is the effect" stance)*: exactly one **EMPTY** leg slot → final calculated MOV is **halved** (rounded up — one leg always beats none; playtest-tunable), stacking deliberately on the DEX-averaging drop; both slots EMPTY → **MOV = 1 flat**, a hard override of base/band/weight/everything ("no legs" is categorical — the ±1 DEX band can't express it). A prosthetic-filled slot counts as a functional leg: the throttle keys on empty slots, not maim history.
- **Multi-maim is allowed** (`maimed_part` becomes a set of flags) and **never escalates to death** — a fully-maimed unit still just downs; "Will never kills" stays absolute. Half STR, half DEX (and the MOV band that follows), MOV pinned to 1 (both legs gone — the leg throttle above), and no verbs is its own signal to bench and repair.
- **Which limb: fixed rotation, natural limbs first** — placeholder order *weapon arm → off leg → off arm → weapon leg*, always shown on the unit panel ("next at risk"). **Prosthetics are targeted only when no natural limb remains, and a maimed prosthetic is recoverable gear** — the part detaches to inventory (the investment survives; refit between battles). Flesh pays first — equivalent exchange. *Punt recorded:* damage-source-derived limb selection (melee→arm, terrain/AoE→leg) is liked but implementation-heavy — a future candidate, not the plan. **The rotation itself is VERY subject to change (dev, 2026-07-15)** — shipped in #56 as the deterministic placeholder, but expect a redesign or remix if a better selector appears; candidates on the table: tie the pick to each character's **main humor** (story layer), handedness (the weapon-arm convention is `ARM_R` purely because handedness isn't modeled), the damage-source punt above, or some mix. Any replacement must stay deterministic and previewable (Law #1 — "next at risk" is public information). **Tracked as its own design question, [#77](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/77) (2026-07-20)** — folds in the aura-tax tiebreak below too, since #60's item 5 (primary affinity as that tiebreak) turned out to be one candidate answer to this same open question, not a separate one.
- *Captured idea, not a decision (dev, 2026-07-28 — roster entry for [#77](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/77), full note in that issue's comments):* two further selector axes — **facing / angle of attack** (which direction the hit came from), and **physical damage type** (bludgeoning vs stabbing vs slicing). Facing is the cheap one: #25 already made the attacker's chosen facing explicit, the same seam a future backstrike wants, and it supplies the in-fiction handedness answer the `ARM_R`-first convention currently lacks. Physical damage type is **not** a selector change but a new content axis — attacks carry only `elemental_damage_type` today, so it means a new enum authored across every attack resource, and probably wants its own decision (armor resisting bludgeoning differently from slicing) before limb selection rides on it. Both share one wrinkle worth grilling first: an attack-dependent pick can't be previewed on the unit panel standing alone the way a fixed rotation can — "next at risk" would have to move into the per-attack queue preview.
- Calibration goal unchanged: maims are semi-regular (the standing cost of fighting depleted), so **meaningful-but-survivable, never catastrophic**.

CON is torso-bound, never limb-slotted; prosthetic *plating* may buff it, exactly as this rider wanted.

## Transmutation strain — the cross-system rule (2026-07-04)

Brute-force channeling ([transmutation-model-proposal.md](transmutation-model-proposal.md) → *Temper & channeling*) costs recoil HP — and that recoil is a **cost, not damage**: **affordability-gated like the Will down-cost.** If the strain would leave the caster at 0 or below, they *cannot pay* — the option greys out in battle (materia is the designed workaround). Strain never touches `take_damage`, never downs, never maims, never triggers Crisis. No self-down gambit, no 1-HP free-cast exploit, no special case in the damage path. (In-battle affordability greying is NOT the codex — the discovery table never greys anything out, per transmutation doctrine #2.)

**Affordability is a resolver-stage check (audit A7, ratified 2026-07-05):** evaluated against the **threaded hypothetical HP at the cast's slot in the plan** — an earlier queued friendly splash on the caster counts. A cast that becomes unaffordable mid-pass resolves as **skipped-with-reason**, previewed in the queue like any known fizzle (Law #2) — not a BREAK; it's fully predicted. Scope: the HP strain cost only; future materia-burn offsets ride the same check when the materia pass lands.

## Forks — status after the 2026-06-24 grill *(all provisional)*

1. ~~**Persist vs reset**~~ — **RESOLVED ([#8]): persists on `UnitInstance`.**
2. **Individual vs squad pool** — grill landed **per-unit, no pool** (everything designed is per-unit; a pool, if ever added, would be additive). *Provisional.*
3. **Attacking a downed unit** — **kill** (downed units rely on the AI *deprioritizing* them, not on invulnerability). *Provisional.* **AMENDED 2026-08-08 ([#126](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/126)): any *damaging* hit kills. A hit that deals 0 damage no longer finishes a body — it can only move it.** That is the whole mechanism behind pushable bodies: `PlanResolver`'s knockback stage (`_knockback_landing` since #259) refuses to shove a target whose outcome is `KILLED`, so while every hit killed, every attempt to *reposition* a downed unit executed it instead, and the ally died where it lay. The rung is keyed on the damage NUMBER rather than on the attack, because `LethalityRules.predict` has two callers (the resolver at plan time, `Unit.take_damage` at execution) and only the number is in both their hands — so preview and execution reach the answer with no new argument and cannot drift (Law #2). Two consequences owned rather than discovered: an attack whose damage is fully absorbed by DEF also stops finishing a body, and an authored **`AttackData.deals_no_damage`** is what makes a damageless attack reachable at all (see [stats.md](stats.md) → the 0-damage rider). What did NOT change: a 0-damage hit is still a **hit** — states, deposits and on-hit effects all still fire — and an elemental reaction's `damage_bonus` is still real damage, so a Quickened fire carving gusted into a WET body electrocutes it and finishes it (dev, 2026-08-08: that bonus is not part of the transmutation, and avoiding it is the player's job).
4. **Limb-loss scope** — **the maim rung only** (Crisis *kills*, it doesn't maim). *Provisional.*
5. ~~**Naming**~~ — **Will** (kept; it reads *better* under the limb-buffer framing — you spend it to stay whole, and out of it the body pays).
