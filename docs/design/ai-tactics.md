## The doctrine: the AI is a player, all the way down

Law #3 ("AI issues orders exclusively through `SquadManager.queue_action`") was necessary but not sufficient — the fists bug (#78) happened because the AI queued through the right chokepoint while *skipping steps of the player's declare flow* (the `fired_attack` stamp). The rebuilt doctrine closes that class of bug:

- **Pick** — the AI selects among `Unit.get_selectable_attacks()`, then writes the winner to `Unit.active_attack` immediately before `declare()` stamps it, the same slot the player's pick menu writes. **Scoring itself no longer touches that slot (#102):** each candidate is passed directly to the reach/victim/splash queries, which take the attack as a parameter. Probe and declare can't disagree because they name the same object, not because they share mutable state — the old arrangement left a live pick behind that skewed every later read.
- **Declare** — both the player's click handler and the AI build attacks through `AttackAction.declare()`, the one factory that stamps `fired_attack` (Law #2's declare-time snapshot). Bare `create()` is for derived actions only.
- **Queue** — `queue_action` + `actor_can_perform()` stay the backstop behind every builder's own gate.
- **Forecast** — candidate scoring resolves the squad's own plan with the candidate added, through `SquadManager.resolve_hypothetical` (#117; it was `PlanResolver.resolve()` on a throwaway volley until 2026-09-02). Same machinery the queue panel previews, so the AI *cannot drift* from it — lethality, counters, standing reactions and the threaded hypo included. The cost is that this pass is **not** pure the way a bare volley was: it publishes projections onto units, so a scoring pass owes a restore at both ends (see *Attack scoring*).

**Consequence for future work:** anything that changes what a unit can fire or what a hit does (new families, carvings, mod effects, readiness economies, #76's strain gate, elemental changes) reaches the AI with **zero AI-side wiring**. If a feature lands player-side and the AI can't see it, the feature bypassed the player surface — fix the feature, not the AI.

## The predictability contract (read this before making the AI cleverer)

> *"One important thing about this game is that turn to turn, AI actions should be easily predictable. The complexity arises from multi-turn planning sequences that don't always go as expected."*
> — dev, 2026-09-02

**Its real use is as a reason to REJECT sophistication**, which is why it is written down rather than left as taste. The difficulty is meant to live in *multi-turn* consequence — a plan that does not survive contact — not in a single turn whose outcome the player cannot forecast. A scoring refinement that makes one turn harder to predict is therefore a cost, and has to buy something with it.

Two things follow, and both already happened:

- **It names the argument #707's pursuit ruling was already made from.** Rushdown pursues the *nearest* rather than its best target partly because *"it goes for whoever is closest"* is a rule you can bait and funnel, where *"it goes for its best target"* is a computation the player cannot see. That was argued from legibility with no name; this is the name.
- **It is what deleted the scoring bar (#711).** *"The AI should always attack if there is an option to, and if all the options are weighed bad, it has to pick its least bad option"* — a rule with no exceptions to hold in your head, replacing one whose refusals depended on counter math the player cannot do. See *Attack scoring*.

**The tension to know about:** the difficulty tiers the dev has floated — easier / medium / harder, weighing trades better and declining when only losing ones exist — make the AI *harder to model*, pulling directly against this. The resolution is [#710](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/710), the threat preview: it lets the AI get less obvious without getting less predictable, because it draws the answer on screen. **Build order therefore matters — preview before tiers, not after.** Both tiers shipped: the HOVER tier 2026-09-16 (`ThreatField`) and the PLAN tier 2026-09-17 (`AIController.preview_turn`, which stands every unit on its projected cell and runs the real archetypes against that board). So the tiers are now unblocked -- see [visual-clarity.md](visual-clarity.md). **They are built as BANDS since [#1230](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1230)** (2026-10-05) -- a few fixed ones, per unit, with deterministic notches rather than chances, so a player who has read a unit keeps that read. See *Difficulty bands* below.

## Difficulty bands ([#1230](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1230), 2026-10-05)

**What a band is.** An archetype says what a squad WANTS: who it chases, where it stands, which actions it will ever take. An `AIProfile` says how WELL a unit goes about it -- one switch or notch per behaviour the archetypes already had. Three ship, Easy / Medium / Hard, in `Resources/AIProfiles/`; a bespoke unit (a boss) is another file beside them.

**Why so few (the dev, 2026-10-05):** *"it is very important for the player to see different units, be able to get a read on what kind of AI they are after a few turns, and be able to plan their strategy from there, without too many surprises."* So: a handful of fixed bands rather than per-mission tuning, and a bespoke profile is an exception the player must be told about. Bands are invisible in the UI for now; he signals them through his own dialogue (Torv's warnings).

**Per UNIT, never per squad** -- units join and leave squads (*Regrouping*, below). Where a squad decides ONCE for everyone, its **leader's** profile decides (*"certainly, leader decides"*): who the squad fights, the destination, the leader's own seek. Everything a unit does for itself reads its own: its candidates and their score, friendly fire, its member step, hazards, route, rescue walk, back-off and regrouping.

The table is the shipped starting point (*"a good starting point"*) and grows with playtests. **The same in every band:** focus fire (a knob was dropped: turning off "a removal counts once per victim" would make a sloppy unit finish your bodies, harsher rather than easier, and the safe half was invisible in play), members stepping in to hit (ruling 9), Rushdown walking at a defended point, each archetype's identity, and every correctness rule.

**Notches, never dice** (dev: *"notches, that kind of thing"*). A chance would make the threat preview -- which runs this same AI -- show one thing while the turn does another, and would drift a replay. A notch is a level chosen by what the unit bends a rule FOR.

**How a unit names its profile.** By FILE NAME (`Unit.ai_profile`, authored on `ScenarioUnitEntry` beside `must_survive` and outside #177's fork), never by reference -- `ScenarioData.look_preset`'s two reasons. `AIProfiles` is the catalog: an unassigned unit plays `Hard.tres`, and so does a name that no longer resolves, which `BoardLint` reports as BLOCKS since substituting Hard is a different mission. The Unit Editor's *AI band* dropdown sets it; the knobs are edited in the Godot inspector. A recorded run's roster names each unit's profile.

**How a knob is read.** At the rule it governs -- `_score_plan` takes the acting unit's profile, so a marginal's base and candidate are always scored the same way, and the seek, the movement rules and the AI's one group-move door (`AITactics.group_move`) ask their unit's. Two knobs gate twice and each gate alone suffices, measured by mutant: `stabilises_bodies` (the heal is not AIMED at a body, and a save is not COUNTED) and `goes_for_mission_kill` (the term, and the seek's early stop, which only saves the search).

**The class defaults ARE the Hard column**, so a band file stores only where it differs -- and a knob added later lands in every band at its default. Decide its value in each band file in the same diff. Every `tests/ai` suite declares the profile its units play (`AIProfiles.use_fixtures`, enforced by `tests/law/test_ai_suites_declare_a_profile.gd`), because the shipped bands are tuned content: a retune must never redden a case about something else.

**Deferred until the first boss** (on #1230): switching an action off -- which would also need the danger field to stop painting threat for a unit that never attacks -- the Springspear minimum as a profile number, a "never on a hazard" notch and an "ignores friends" friendly-fire notch.

## The policy registry (the one place per-archetype behavior lives)

`AIArchetype` declares, per archetype (the idiom the now-deleted `CRISIS_STANCES` table also used, until #158):

- `MAIN_ACTION_PRIORITY` — an ordered try-list over `BaseAction.MAIN_ACTION_TYPES`; `AITactics.queue_main_action` walks it and the first type that yields a buildable candidate wins.
- `MAIN_ACTION_NEVER` — the explicit opt-outs.

Every main action type must land in exactly one of the two, for every archetype — pinned by `tests/law/test_ai_action_coverage.gd`. **A new verb cannot silently skip the AI**: the suite stays red until a stance is declared, even if that stance is NEVER. This is the action registry's AI column, mirroring how `test_action_registry.gd` pins the pipeline.

Candidate builders live in `AITactics` (one per type, each mirroring `MainActionMenu`'s gate for that verb); an undeclared builder is a loud `push_error`, never a silent skip. **The table says WHAT an archetype will ever do; WHEN a weapon verb is worth doing is the family's own call** — `queue_main_action` asks the equipped weapon's `AIWeaponRoutine` before a weapon verb's builder runs (see *Weapon routines*).

**An archetype outlives the squad for anyone who leaves it** ([#1196](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1196), dev 2026-10-03). A unit ejected from its squad (downed, out of contact, dropped by a new leader) or disbanded keeps the old squad's archetype and zone in its solo squad. Before this it read `FACTION_DEFAULT`, i.e. Rushdown, so a Hold squad that rescued its own member got back a unit that charged the player. The post is not copied: `SentryArchetype` gives a solo Sentry a fresh one at its first turn.

Until then, situational behaviour is a **refusal** (#726's shape), not a weight — and his own example already works that way: a dry Carbine reloads because ATTACK yields no candidate at all, never because the order changed.

### The two standing-reaction rules

Both verbs are **preparations**, so both are rules rather than score terms — the ward absorbs and the watch fires on somebody *else's* turn, which `_score_plan` structurally cannot reach. That is #726's doctrine reaching its second application, and it is why neither preempts ATTACK.

**That REASON went half-false at [#1003](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1003) (2026-09-17) and the rule stands anyway.** A watch armed onto a cell an enemy is already standing in fires *in this pass*, so the watch is reachable by a score after all — but the doctrine's other half is the load-bearing one: a preparation is a legible sentence, and the predictability contract prefers one to a score term. Worth knowing before touching `_watch_aim`: it floods **from the enemy's own cell**, so a lane containing that cell scores zero hops and wins the ranking outright — the AI already prefers aiming where the enemy is standing, and that aim is now a shot.

**ATTACK sitting above OVERWATCH does NOT keep that shot rare ([#1197](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1197), 2026-10-03).** This paragraph used to say it did, "since a lane the enemy occupies is by definition somewhere the same attack could have been fired". That stopped being true at [#590](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/590), which made a watch attack never fireable: the fire view and the watch view are disjoint. So ATTACK above OVERWATCH only keeps the shot rare where the two happen to cover the same cells. The Carbine is the live counterexample: its Shot fires at exactly 2 and its watch lane runs 1 to 4. A Hold or Sentry Carbine with nothing at range 2 therefore arms a watch over a neighbour and shoots it on the spot. In practice the watch is that unit's point-blank shot. Whether it SHOULD be is [#752](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/752)'s question and is not decided here. What #1197 fixed is the preview, which painted that cell safe. `ThreatField` now draws every lane a watcher could set next turn, through `AITactics.watch_attack_for` and `watch_lanes`, the two doors `_try_overwatch` itself uses; see visual-clarity.md → *The field draws the watch shot and the current*.

- **A watcher aims down the way an enemy would COME, and what it aims is the whole LANE.** Routes are walked from the **enemy's** side with its own traversal, so the rule is true around a wall rather than along a straight line — **and that walk SEES THROUGH BODIES ([#946](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/946), 2026-09-13)**, which is the one place this differs from the approach picker below. It ran with occupancy on until then, inherited from #751; #769 (next clause) made an occupied NEAR CELL cost one cell instead of a facing, and the WHOLE-FIELD form survived that — a body on a chokepoint seals the map, so four archers aimed into a fence because three of their own squadmates stood in the board's only ford two hundred cells away. `can_traverse` states the doctrine (*"an enemy body blocks a MOVE but is not a terrain fact and moves every turn; a connectivity field must see through it"*) and the TENSE settles it here: a watch stands until the watcher's faction's next turn, so every body the field would refuse to see through has moved before the watch can fire. The approach picker keeps occupancy on for the opposite reason — it is estimating a route a unit walks THIS turn. **The distance is measured to the nearest cell of the FOOTPRINT, never to the cell in front ([#769](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/769))** — a watch fires when an enemy enters *any* cell it covers, so that is what the route question has to be asked about; between two lanes the enemy reaches equally soon the one covering MORE reachable cells wins, then `CARDINAL_DIRECTIONS` order, and a sealed board falls back to simply facing them through `GridUtils`' own diagonal tie-break — **which may not aim at a wall (#946)**: that hatch guarded only against an EMPTY lane, and a lane #756 truncated to the one impassable cell beside the shooter is not empty. A watch fires when an enemy MOVES INTO a covered cell, so a lane holding nothing anybody may stand on can never fire however it is pointed; the ranked pass gets that test for free (`path_hops` cannot put an untraversable cell in its field, so such a facing already covers zero), and the fallback, which consults no field, asks it out loud. No facing left means no watch, and the unit takes the next verb in its archetype's list. Reading `origin + dir` alone was **#127's shape a third time** — a metric measuring one cell of the thing it ranks — and it cost three answers: a lane #756 had truncated to a stub tied with a whole one; a facing whose near cell was merely OCCUPIED was refused outright, and since the walk runs from the enemy's side with occupancy on, the blocker is usually the watcher's own squadmate; and a diagonal approach tied every facing at its near cell and fell through to cardinal order, watching north while the enemy walked in from the east. **The aim is ACTIVE-only, and that is #720's pathology one door over**: `nearest_enemy` ranks a body as an ordinary target and it wins on route, but `_watch_triggered_by` refuses a non-ACTIVE entrant and a corpse never moves — so the unfiltered answer aims at an approach nobody can ever walk. **Who stands in the lane is not asked** (dev, 2026-09-05: *"the overwatch is an attack"*) — an ally-hitting watch shoots its own squad, and that is the attack behaving as authored.
- **A guard wards the ally the most enemies could reach and hit** — one move-range search per enemy, reused across candidates, and **zero exposure REFUSES**: "most exposed" presumes exposure above zero, and without the refusal a ward would spend the action protecting nobody every time an ally happened to stand adjacent.
- **Both decide on the board the squad's plan leaves ([#1209](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1209)).** They run after the squad's attacks are queued. So an enemy a squadmate fells this turn is neither watched for nor counted as a threat, and one a squadmate shoves is measured from where it lands. See *The MOVEMENT layer still measures to LIVE enemy cells* below for why the movement layer itself still doesn't need this.

**A dud aim must never be queued, and nothing downstream would catch one.** A hint yielding no cardinal direction makes a self-anchored attack answer with no cells, and from there the failure is entirely silent — the resolver arms nothing, `Unit.arm_watch` refuses an empty footprint, and no queue gate inspects an `OverwatchAction` at all. The unit would have spent its main action on air behind a legal-looking row, which is why the builder guarantees a real facing and the tests assert on the **footprint**, never on the queue row.

**Declared limit, seen in the first replay:** a watch armed while everyone is still far away lapses unused at the owner's next turn. It costs nothing that was being spent — those Carbines idled before — but it is not free attention either, and the fix if it wants one is a refusal (*don't watch when nobody can reach the footprint before it lapses*), which the hop count the aim already computes would answer.

- Rescue before Reload: a returned unit now beats rearming for later. (The verb was `SPRING_LOAD` when these tables were ratified; it went generic as `RELOAD` in #84 when the Carbine wanted the same order — same slot, same priority, one more family using it.)
- **REV was Rushdown-only from 2026-08-06 (AI generalization sweep, finding #3)** — before that it had been
  `NEVER` for every archetype with no builder at all (unreachable even if declared — the
  `queue_main_action` match had no arm for it). `AITactics._try_rev` mirrors `_try_reload`

  **Hold/Sentry gained REV and BURROW on 2026-09-03 ([#726](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/726), dev: a defender at its post revs, digs in, tops off)** (slotted ahead of INTIMIDATE, since retired); `_try_burrow` is the rev pair's

## Shared engage plumbing

`AITactics.engage(squad, target, board, squad_manager, allowed = null, within = null, weigh_safety = false)` is the one place "fight
this target" is defined: destination pick (`best_attack_destination`) → the seek (`seek_positions`, see the next section) → conditional group move
→ every member tries a main action. Rushdown's whole turn and Sentry's intruder branch both call
it. It had been hand-duplicated between the two files since #29 — flagged by the #127 handoff
(2026-08-06) as the shape that let that fix's destination-picker half reach both archetypes for
free, since it landed one layer down in `AITactics` rather than in either archetype file;
extracted the same day. `weigh_safety` is Balanced's alone (#1220 ruling 11): the approach's last tie-break before distance becomes how many enemies could reach the cell next turn.

The per-member "everybody tries a main action" step is itself `AITactics.queue_main_actions_for_squad(squad, board, squad_manager)`
— `engage()`'s tail, the whole of `HoldArchetype`'s turn, and (since finding #3, same day)
Rushdown's own no-enemy branch, which used to `return` above it and skip fallback main actions
(Reload/Rev) entirely whenever the squad found no target at all (`choose_engagement_target` since
2026-09-02; `nearest_enemy` before it). `HoldArchetype` never moves, so it has no destination/move step, only this one.

## Seeking a removal or a squad break ([#760](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/760), 2026-10-03)

The AI used to take a void kill only when it happened to be standing in the right place. The destination pick chooses where the leader stands before any attack is scored, the formation solver places the members by offset, and the attack pass then works from wherever everyone ended up. So a kill one step away was never even built as a candidate. `AITactics.seek_positions` runs inside `engage()`, between the destination pick and the group move, and decides where people stand.

**The dev's rulings (2026-10-03, recorded on #760):**
- **Only a removal or a squad break pulls a unit off its usual cell.** Those are the two terms the score ranks above damage. Damage alone never moves anyone, so all other positioning is what it was. This was chosen over "a removal only" and over "its best attack by full score". The rule reads as one sentence: *if it can knock you off, out or into something this turn, it will.*
  - **REPEALED FOR MEMBERS by [#1220](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1220) (ruling 9, 2026-10-04)**: *"If it can hit you, it will, but it should not go as far as leaving the squad to do so."* A member with nobody to hit from its formation cell steps to a cell inside the leash it can hit from. The leader is still moved only by a removal, a break or the mission. See *Members who find nothing* below.
  - **The mission sits above both** (ruling 2): a cell from which the plan kills a protected unit whose death ends the mission is the seek's top tier (`AITactics.Tier.MISSION`), so a leader walks to finish the VIP.
- **Every member seeks, and the squad is one unit.** His words: *"First priority squad leader, second priority closest member that can do it, etc."*, and *"we have a lot of heuristics to make this not as big of a calculation."*

**How it works:**
- **The leader goes first**, because its cell decides everyone's cohesion.
  - It searches its move range, inside the leash (`allowed`), on cells its squad can follow it to (`GroupMoveSolver.followable_destinations`) and nobody else stands on.
  - The order is its default destination first, then cheapest to reach, then row-major.
- **Members go in rounds.** Each round:
  - the formation solver places everyone not yet assigned;
  - every unassigned member searches its own cells (`GroupMoveSolver.follow_cells`), its formation cell first;
  - the winner is a removal over a break, then the **lowest move cost**, then member order;
  - the winner is **pinned** (`GroupMoveSolver.plan(…, pinned)`), so the formation places everyone else around it.
  - Each member scores against what the earlier winners will already do, so two never chase one body.
- **The first removal ends a unit's search.** A squad break is kept only as a fallback while a removal is still being looked for.
- **A Sentry counts only a candidate aimed at an intruder** (`within`), so bait outside the zone cannot pull it.
- **The attack is still the joint pass's to choose** (*Attack scoring* below). The seek only decides where people stand.

**A cell is scored by standing the unit on it.** This uses the threat preview's positional snapshot, through `MovementComponent.set_cell`, and it is the *only* way.
- The resolve re-expands every queued aim from its **actor's** projected cell (#15). It ignores the origin the aim was declared with, so an aim "from" a cell the actor is not on resolves as nothing.
- #760's own body claimed the opposite ("an attack carries its own origin through the whole resolve"). That holds for the knockback and elevation reads, but the volley itself is rebuilt from the actor, so the claim was half stale. The issue's counter question has the same answer.
- With the squad's queue empty, a unit set on a cell projects to it, so the volley, the counters and the lethality all read the cell it would really fire from.
- This is honest **only while nothing is queued**. That is true at every `engage()` call, because the hand-off drain sheds orders and engage runs first. With orders queued, `seek_positions` returns the default instead.
- Every cell is put back and the real plan re-resolved before anything is queued.

**Heuristics that bound the cost:**
1. **The priority is the search order.** The leader first, then the closest member, then each unit's default cell before any other. The first removal stops that unit's search.
2. **One resolve per outcome-equivalent group** (`_group_key`).
   - The resolver reads three things off an attacker's cell: the shove direction, the height, and a carving's empowerment. Cells that agree on those, on the attack and on the aim give a single victim the same fate.
   - A directional attack's footprint moves with its origin, so for those the cell itself joins the key.
   - Once a group's first resolve shows its one victim neither removed nor broken, the group is skipped.
   - Counters differ per cell, so a group whose fate is good but whose first cell nets a loss tries its next cell.
   - **A rule that ever reads more of the origin (backstrike) has to join the key**, or the seek skips a real opportunity without a word.
3. **Only fights seek.** Hold never moves. A Sentry at its post or walking home does not seek, and neither does Rushdown's cargo run.
4. **Geometry before resolves.** A cell from which nothing can be aimed costs no resolve.

**A trial's shove stays PUBLISHED** (#709's trap again). The candidate builder reads published positions, so the base plan is re-resolved before the next cell's candidates are built. The first build missed this: after the default cell's trial, the target projected one cell east and nothing was aimable from the cell that would have dropped it in the hole.

**Measured** (`tools/profile_ai_turn.gd`, Castle Assault, against the branch with the seek short-circuited):
- The whole-board decision went from about 1134 ms to about 1254 ms, **+10%**, against a 50% budget.
- Castle Assault carries no shoves and its decision record is byte-identical, so that number is cost alone.
- Moles and Holes: +3% on the opening board. A four-round AI-vs-AI replay is decision-identical too, apart from squad id numbering; its Gust void kill happens either way.
- **No shipped board has yet shown a detour.** `tests/ai/test_shove_seeking.gd` carries the behaviour.

**Declared limits:**
- ~~**The walk to the cell is not priced:** a watch crossed on the way, or fire at the end of it.~~ **Both answered since #1220.** The walk takes the route that wakes the fewest watches ([#920](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/920), `RulesService.route_to`), and no cell is ended on a hazard while a safe one offers the same (below). Fire crossed on the way still costs nothing, because fire hurts only where you stop ([#509](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/509) is unbuilt).
- **The leader's search sees its followers where they stand now**, not where the formation will put them.
- **A cell another unit stands on is not searched by the leader**, a squadmate's included, even though that squadmate would move.

### Members who find nothing, and hazards ([#1220](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1220), 2026-10-05)

After the seek's rounds, every member nobody pinned is asked, in member order, what it does from its formation cell. **Formation plus pins** (ruling 10): the answer is always a pin on #760's machinery, never a per-unit model.

- **It can hit someone there:** it stays.
- **It can hit someone from another follow cell** (ruling 9): it is pinned to the first such cell in `GroupMoveSolver.follow_cells` order, so it never leaves the leash. Judged by GEOMETRY, with no resolve (`_hits_from`, the candidate builder's own answer), which is what keeps it cheap.
- **It can hit nobody from any follow cell** (ruling 12): if its archetype's list has RESCUE and it can carry, it walks beside the most urgent body (lowest downed clock), one rescuer per body.
- **It has NOTHING TO FIRE** (ruling 5; `get_selectable_attacks()` empty -- literally nothing, not merely nothing in range): it rescues first under the same conditions, else it falls back to the follow cell the fewest enemies could reach, read off `ThreatField.build` from the AI's own side and built once per squad decision, lazily. A dry LEADER of an armed squad keeps leading; leading nobody armed, it falls back the same way.
- **Hold never moves** (ruling 17): none of this runs for Hold, which has no movement step at all.

**No cell is ended on a HAZARD while a safe one offers the same** (rulings 15 and 19). A hazard is a cell that takes end-of-turn damage (`RulesService.occupant_damage_for`) or that a hostile watch would fire on (`PlanResolver.watch_fires_at`, the resolve's own predicate); the cell a unit stands on is never a watch hazard. Safe cells are searched first, WITHIN a tier: a kill from a burning tile still beats a scratch from safe ground, but damage is never taken from fire while a safe firing cell exists. It is applied at the three places a cell is chosen: the approach ladder (`_approach_beats`), the seek's search order, and `GroupMoveSolver`'s placement (a per-member hazard set, optional, so the player's own preview is untouched).

Its second half stands alone as `AITactics.queue_fallback_actions_for_squad` — the per-member walk with ATTACK removed — because **a Sentry AT ITS POST with nobody in the zone takes exactly that and never an attack** ([#726](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/726), dev 2026-09-03: *"at the post only"*). An enemy in reach but outside the zone is bait, and the lure-proofing contract is unchanged (`test_cannot_be_lured_by_enemy_in_reach_but_outside_zone` now asserts no move and no attack rather than an empty queue). Before this the branch queued nothing at all, so an idle sentry never reloaded either — the "Carbine needed zero AI work" story was false for one archetype. The walk-home branch stays silent on purpose: entrenching a cell you are leaving is waste.

**A FACTION TURN RE-DERIVES THE BOARD PER SQUAD** ([#714](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/714), 2026-09-03). `AIController.take_faction_turn` takes no `BoardContext` parameter, and that absence is the fix: it used to build one for the whole turn while `execute_orders` between squads spans frames, so a unit an earlier squad **killed** was genuinely freed by the time a later squad planned — and `SquadManager._resolve_actions` opens by calling a method on every unit the board lists. The crash was the loud half; for the rest of that turn the AI had also been targeting, pathing and measuring cohesion against a roster including the dead.

**THE FIRST SQUAD PLANS BEHIND THE BANNER** (#1220 ruling 7, *"hide the cost"*). An AI faction claims the board (`AI_TURN` and the playback lock) BEFORE the turn hand-off beat, waits one frame so the banner draws, plans its first squad, and waits out what the beat has left; the loop then skips that squad's planning. Claiming first is not optional: planning queues orders, and an unlocked board would let the player click them. Only the first squad can hide: no camera pan precedes planning since #987, and planning cannot run on a thread. Measured on Castle Assault: a squad decides in roughly 250-500 ms, so the first one now lands inside the 1.0 s beat and every later squad still stalls between passes. `planned_squad_count` and `handoff_squad` are the wire's only headless observables, a headless beat lasting no time.

Two things are worth carrying from it. **`play_session._take_ai_turn` had always called `_board()` inside its own loop** — two live implementations of one walk, with the fault in whichever was not the model, which is the same shape as the `went_downed` wire that was missing from the game and present in the Play API for thirteen months. And **`BoardContext`'s constructor now excludes a unit whose death has RESOLVED**, asking the domain fact (`Unit.die()` sets lifecycle DEAD *before* it queue_frees) rather than `is_queued_for_deletion()`; the filter is in the constructor because three builders feed it and a rule in one is a rule the other two disagree with.

## Attack scoring (ratified 2026-07-22; rebuilt onto the SQUAD'S PLAN 2026-09-02, #117)

A candidate is scored by its **marginal gain to the squad's plan** — `score(plan + candidate) − score(plan)`, both sides resolved by the real `SquadManager.resolve_plan` machinery — as an `AIScore` -- `(mission, removals, splits, saves, limbs, net damage, damage taken)`, compared lexicographically, with less damage taken the better. **The score ORDERS candidates; it gates one only to keep a squad from felling its own** (#711 below, and #1220's floor). *(Since [#1220](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1220), 2026-10-05, the score is a small comparable type rather than a vector, because it grew to six terms; every `.x`/`.y` reader went with it. Before that it was `(net removals, net squad breaks, net damage dealt, −damage taken from reactions)`.)* *(Two terms until the third landed with target selection, 2026-09-02 — see* Target selection *for why it is a tie-break rather than a cost. The squad-break term joined second with [#761](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/761), 2026-10-03, so a tuple quoted below from before that date is three terms: removals, damage, taken.)*

**The #1220 terms (dev rulings 1-3 and 16, 2026-10-04):**

- **ENDING THE MISSION OUTRANKS EVERYTHING** (ruling 2). A plan that kills a `must_survive` unit (#572), when the board's mission carries `PROTECTED_UNIT_LOST`, scores a mission point: a downed VIP finished, or a standing one killed in a blow. `BoardContext` carries the `MissionState` for this, from all three builders. It is also the seek's top tier, so a leader walks to take it.
- **THE AI NEVER FELLS ITS OWN** (ruling 1, replacing #711's provisional no-floor). A candidate whose OWN rows newly fell a unit on our side, measured against the plan without it, is refused: its volley, and anyone its deposits drowned (`plan.sinks`, ice melted from under a squadmate). A COUNTER it draws is not its work, so *a free finish beats a suicidal swing* still stands, and knocking our own into CRISIS counts as a fall whatever the unit sees, since it spends their one safety net. A unit whose profile is NEVER_KILLS (Easy) may down one of its own, never kill one (#1230). The same floor sits in the lookahead's pair and in the seek. Chip damage on a squadmate is still the soft penalty below.
- **A HEAL IS PRICED LIKE DAMAGE** (ruling 3). HP actually restored on our side adds to net damage, HP a heal's splash gives an enemy subtracts, and overheal is worth nothing. A heal is never AIMED at an enemy.
- **A HEAL ON A BODY IS A SAVE, and only when nobody can rescue it** (ruling 16). A heal stabilises a downed ally (#1002 stops the clock), and that is priced as a death prevented -- the `saves` term, above damage and below a squad break -- but only for a body no squadmate could rescue this turn (RESCUE in its archetype's list, able to carry, beside it). Who could rescue is `_Stakes`, built once per squad decision.
- **A drowning counts as damage dealt.** A unit the plan's deposits sank (`plan.sinks`) joins the ledger like any victim.
- **TAKING A LIMB OUTRANKS DAMAGE, both ways** ([#1230](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1230), dev 2026-10-05; `values_limbs`, on in Hard). The `limbs` term, below saves, counts `resolved.severed_limb` off attacks, counters and watch shots: +1 for an enemy's, -1 for one of ours, so a counter that would take one of the AI's limbs counts against the attack. **Only a victim standing at plan start counts** -- a body takes a limb at a Wounded 8, and a limb on a body must not outrank damage on someone standing (#720). A kill never severs; a Crisis entry that severs does. The seek's tiers are unchanged, the limb ranking below them.

- **BREAKING A SQUAD IS WORTH MORE THAN DAMAGE AND LESS THAN A REMOVAL** *(dev rulings, 2026-10-03, [#761](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/761), chosen over a tie-break below damage and over a tunable damage-equivalent knob)*. The consequence was stated when he chose it: a zero-damage shove that knocks somebody out of their squad beats a damaging hit that does not. **The term is counted off `SplitForecast`**, the forecast behind the queue's Split chip (#367), so the AI prices exactly the settle execution runs; `SquadManager.resolve_hypothetical` stamps it now as well as `resolve_plan`, and `SplitForecast.leavers(plan)` is the one read. It is **per unit and symmetric**: a leader shoved away from three members is +3, and a counter that shoves one of OURS out of our squad is −1. **A unit the plan also removes is skipped**, because the forecast lists a downed unit's ejection and the removal already paid for it. **Declared limit:** the AI prices a split from the cell it already stands on and never walks to one — going looking for a shove (a cliff, a void, a split) is [#760](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/760), a positioning question rather than a scoring one. Fall damage, void removals and drownings were already priced through `PlanResolver` before this term existed.

Until #117 the throwaway plan held **one volley against the live board**, so a member could not see what its squadmates had already committed to. That is what made three things impossible rather than merely unwise: a second member re-spent its action on a target the first already downed, a finishing blow looked like any other hit, and a damageless set-up (Splash) scored `(0,0)` and was refused outright — the AI was **structurally unable to open a combo**.

- Active enemies count **for**, any ally **against**, symmetrically — friendly fire is a soft penalty, not a ban (net-damage doctrine). Since #711 that penalty ORDERS rather than vetoes: a clean attack outranks one that splashes your own line, but with no clean option the splash is still taken. **Felling one of our own is the exception since #1220** (the floor above): the splash may scratch a squadmate, never drop one.
- **A REMOVAL IS PER VICTIM, NOT PER HIT** — "on its feet before the plan, off them after": a change of LIFECYCLE for a unit that sees Crisis, and `PlanResolver.plan_fells` for one blind to it (#1230). Counting the lethality rung of each row instead double-pays: the ladder answers `KILLED` for *any* damaging hit on a downed body, so the second member swinging at someone the first downed banked a fresh removal. **CRISIS lands ACTIVE and used to fall out as nothing on those grounds; since [#708](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/708) a blind unit counts it as a removal anyway, and since #1230 a unit that sees Crisis does not** — see *Crisis, by profile* below. `plan_fells` is the seam that already answered both (it is #313's alarm predicate), so the AI's removal and the health bar's alarm are one question rather than two; the `is_active()` guard stays at the caller, which is what keeps a body from earning one.
- **OVERKILL IS WORTH NOTHING** — a victim's damage is capped at the HP they had going in. Without this the removal ledger fixes only half the double-spend: full damage on an already-downed body ties exactly with the same number on an untouched enemy, leaving focus-fire to board order. The cap binds only on overkill.
- **A REACTION'S DAMAGE IS NOT SCORED, ONLY ITS REMOVALS** *(dev ruling, 2026-09-02)*. The plan now derives counters and watch shots, which is what closes *counters aren't scored* — but priced at par a counter cancels exactly: two units with the same weapon trade 3 for 3, **every even exchange scores `(0,0)`**, and a squad facing mirror-statted enemies declines every attack and reloads instead. Removals are the currency and damage the tie-break, so a counter that *fells* one of ours is a real loss and a scratch is the price of engaging. Worth knowing before re-opening this: C1/C4 make the counter a **per-plan fixed cost** that the *opener* bears alone (`defender_groups_that_countered` gates per squad, and every member of that party reacts to the first attack against it), so at par the AI would not merely decline even trades — it would never open on a multi-member squad at all.
- ~~**Felling someone still standing always beats finishing a body or overkilling.**~~ *(dev, 2026-09-02 — the reason was the player's rescue window.)* **SUPERSEDED by [#720](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/720) (2026-09-03): the same priority, kept as a RANKING and dropped as a GATE.** His words, from playtest: *"It should be the same level of prioritization as other attacks, it just loses to other attacks in the head to head."* #57's two passes were that rule as a hard precedence — a body neither aimed at nor scored while anyone stood in reach — and one pass over every target replaces them, because **the arithmetic was already saying it**: a downed unit clings at 1 HP (`Unit._go_downed`), so the overkill clamp prices finishing one at exactly **+1** — and since [#1002](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1002) a heal can raise that HP, so the clamp caps a body at 1 OUTRIGHT rather than at what it holds, which is what keeps this ruling literal and `_plan_removes` answers false for a body, so it cannot earn a removal either. It loses to any real swing without a gate on top. What the gate added was thirteen rounds of Castle Assault in which five attackers revved beside a downed general — see *Target selection*, since the layer that failed was the one that decides where to STAND. Overkill is the same rule read through the same clamp: a second swing at someone the plan already felled is worth `(0,0)`, ranks below any fresh target, and — since #711 removed the bar — is taken anyway when nothing else is in reach.
  - **THE ONE COMPARISON A BODY WINS, and it is a ruling rather than a side effect** *(dev, 2026-09-03)*: a body in reach beside a standing target whose counter would FELL the attacker. The two rulings point opposite ways there, and **the score decides** — a free finish at `(0,+1,0)` against a suicidal swing at `(-1,d,-x)`, where a removal on our own side is the first term. Pinned by `test_a_free_finish_outranks_a_swing_that_would_fell_the_attacker`, which asserted the opposite until #720.
  - **A TARGET THE PLAN HAS ALREADY KILLED IS NOT A TARGET** ([#719](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/719), 2026-09-03). Planning does not execute, so a unit a squadmate felled *this round* is still `is_active()` on the live board; the candidate filter asks `base_plan.hypo` instead, which is also why `_best_candidate_for`/`_lookahead` take the base **plan** rather than its score. Pass 1 had asked it since #117 and **pass 2 never did** — one filter with the clause and one without, drifting exactly as #716's duplicate did — which is what queued a leader's 6-long `hits_allies` line straight down her own party at a corpse, marginal `(0,-20,0)`. Hypo-**DOWNED** is still a target, because finishing is intended; hypo-**DEAD** is not, because there is nobody there to finish.
  - **AND THE AIM FOLLOWS THE PLAN'S OWN SHOVE** ([#709](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/709), 2026-09-04). The four reads that build a candidate — reach test, vertical gate, footprint, declared aim — took `other.movement.cell`, so once anything in the plan displaced a target, every remaining candidate against it was built at a cell nobody was on. They ask `PlanResolver.projected_position(other, base_hypo)` now, the sibling of the lifecycle read one line above and answered off the same dict. **The cost was NOT the whiff the ticket predicted**, which is worth knowing before re-reading it: `gather_attack_victims` already resolved occupants projected, so such a candidate came back with an empty victim list and was DROPPED at that guard — never built, never scored, never queued, never refused. What was lost was the OPTION, and the direction that was wholly invisible is the other one: a shove that pulls a target INTO a squadmate's reach produced no candidate at all, because `reach.has(other.movement.cell)` gates first. #258's *"(Live cell, not projected — the declared v1 approximation.)"* on the vertical gate goes with it — the gate has to test the cell the attack aims at.

  - ~~**Pass 2 needs an EMPTY pass 1, not a losing one.**~~ **SUPERSEDED by #711 (2026-09-03), and vacuous rather than wrong** — with the bar gone pass 1 had no losing outcome, so the `considered` bookkeeping went as dead code; #720 then deleted the passes themselves. **The half that survives is REFUSAL**: a candidate `queue_action` turned down is skipped before it can count, so it was never a real option.
  - ~~The base score is measured on the same `include_downed` terms as the candidate.~~ **GONE with the fork it existed for ([#716](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/716), 2026-09-03)**, and the fork itself went a day later: there is one pass, one base, one list of candidates.

**A DOWNED VICTIM IN THE BLAST IS PRICED LIKE ANY OTHER** *(dev ruling, 2026-09-03, from playtest: a general with an AoE had two options that both hit an upright enemy, only one of which also caught a downed one, and he took the one that hit nobody extra — "it felt like the AI was playing for the wrong team")*. The score used to skip damage on an already-downed victim during pass 1, which erased the incidental value of an attack that catches a corpse on the way to a standing target. **Its justification has since gone stale while the rule stayed right** — it read *"a second spelling of the precedence directly above: `_attack_candidates` already refuses to aim at a body while anyone is standing"*, and #720 deleted that precedence a day later. What keeps a body from outranking somebody upright is now the arithmetic below, not a gate above it. His ruling: *prioritize the standing, but never DE-prioritize an otherwise better attack for having a body in it.*

The magnitude is self-limiting rather than tuned, which is why no knob was needed: a downed unit clings at 1 HP (`Unit._go_downed`) — and where a heal has raised it, the clamp caps a body at 1 anyway (#1002) — so the overkill clamp values finishing one at exactly **+1 damage** — enough to break a tie, never enough to outrank felling someone on their feet — and `_plan_removes` still answers false for a body, so an incidental corpse cannot earn a removal either.
- Ties: earlier member, then earlier candidate — attacks in `get_selectable_attacks()` order, then units in board order. Deterministic, Law #1.
- No selectable attacks (unarmed, or an aura-dry rune) → **no candidate at all**, matching the player's ring, which offers no attack row in the same state ([#1215](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1215), dev ruling 2026-10-04: *the AI loses the punch*). This reverses #78's null-pick probe, which matched the player when it was written. #102 then closed the player's generic Attack row for both states as a side effect of an unrelated fix, and the AI kept punching. The danger field, Guard's exposure count and the Play API agree: nothing to fire, nothing threatened. The resolver still reads a null stamp as bare fists; no surface chooses one.

**CRISIS, BY PROFILE** *([#1230](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1230), dev 2026-10-05 -- reversing #708's blindness, which he calls a scope call of its time).* A unit that SEES Crisis (`sees_crisis`, on in Hard) prices a hit that sets the gambit off as the HP it really takes -- down to `CRISIS_REVIVE_HP`, never below zero -- and NO removal, since the target stands straight back up. A unit already IN Crisis stays an ordinary target: its next drop is a kill, and a kill is a removal. The same pricing reaches counters and sinks (`AITactics._hp_cost`), so what a counter that sets off OUR Crisis costs is measured the same way. A unit BLIND to it keeps #708's rule, which follows.

**A UNIT BLIND TO CRISIS** *(dev ruling, 2026-09-04 — [#708](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/708); Easy and Medium since #1230)*. His words: *"just plug in the damage an attack would have done if crisis mode didn't exist in those cases. Simply have the AI ignore it… for now, the ai simply won't see crisis mode until they have to react to a unit currently in it."* A hit the ladder sentences to CRISIS is priced at the damage **and** the removal it would have earned without the gambit.

**What it replaced was not neutrality but a penalty.** The damage was skipped outright and CRISIS threads ACTIVE so no removal followed, giving `(0,0,0)` — and since #711 deleted the bar, a zero is not a refusal, it is a *losing* candidate. Any ordinary target outranked it, so a full-Will Berserker was the last thing an AI would swing at. #708 filed that as *"a neutral verdict means a refusal"*, which was true only while the bar existed; the ranking half is what survived and what this fixes.

**Neither half needed new arithmetic, and that is worth knowing before re-opening it.** `outcome.damage` is fixed before the rung is named (the Crisis branch rewrites only `hp`/`will`/`in_crisis`), so the raw pre-Crisis number is already there, and the overkill clamp caps it at the HP the victim had going in — which a would-be-down met by definition. The removal came from pointing `_plan_removes` at **`PlanResolver.plan_fells`**, which already answered *"did this pass move the rung, counting a fresh Crisis"* for #313's alarm. One question, one answer; the `is_active()` guard stays at the caller so a body still earns nothing.

- **BLIND TO THE GAMBIT, SIGHTED TO WHAT IT DRAWS** *(dev, same day, asked as its own fork)*. A Crisis'd defender is still ACTIVE, so it **counters** where a downed one cannot, and that counter's damage still lands in `z`. Between two otherwise identical targets the AI therefore still prefers the one that cannot answer. Declared rather than accidental: blinding `z` as well is a separate edit in the reaction loop, and *"it takes the finishing blow, even into a Crisis"* is the legible sentence the predictability contract asks for — an AI that also ignored the reply would be harder to model, not easier.
- **Consequences to expect rather than re-file, for a blind unit.** A follow-up on a target the plan already Crisis'd prices at `(0,0)`, so the AI leaves it standing at `CRISIS_REVIVE_HP` with a surge pending until next turn. A counter that would trigger **our** Berserker's Crisis now scores as a removal against us, so the AI steers away from arming its own gambit — the opposite of what a smarter AI would want, and part of what "provisional" is covering. And the Springspear routine reads the marginal removal term (`score.x` then, `score.removals` since #1220) for *fells*, so a Spring that triggers a Crisis now reads as a kill and is no longer deferred.

### Where a candidate aims ([#1220](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1220), with [#1207](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1207))

`_attack_candidates` used to aim each attack AT each enemy. Three things that could not express are gone:

- **A DIRECTIONAL attack tries all four facings**, aimed one step out the way the watch lanes are, because a facing is what the player picks. Aiming at an enemy asked for the cardinal between two cells, which named the wrong facing for a spread's side lane.
- **A POINT attack tries the cells of its ring**, so a placed blast can be dropped beside a target rather than on it. A one-cell attack with nothing that pays far from its footprint (no payload, no current) skips empty cells exactly, so it pays nothing for the widening.
- **A HEAL is aimed at allies** (itself when `hits_self`), never at an enemy: an ally below full HP, or a body under ruling 16's condition.

An aim is kept only when its sweep pays -- a hostile victim, or for a heal a heal target -- and aims that reach the same victims across the same cells are one candidate. **A MAP-ONLY attack** hits no unit (#1135), so its aim is kept only for what it does THIS pass: the current it carries, the payloads it drops, or a hostile on frozen deep water whose ice it melts. **The sweep reads wetness LIVE**, as the queue's whiff gate does (`SquadPlanValidator.aim_finds_a_target`), so a candidate is never one the gate would refuse.

**The field had to widen with it (#1207).** `_squad_can_reach_anyone` trusts the danger field to be an upper bound on what the AI can aim at, so `ThreatField._add_splash` dilates a placed blast's stamp and a payload's landing (knockback, then the payload's own footprint) onto the ring.

**Selection is JOINT.**
 `AITactics._queue_attacks_jointly` runs in rounds: re-resolve the squad's real plan, score every remaining member's every candidate against it, queue the single best, repeat until every member that can attack has. (It ran "until nothing beats `(0,0)`" until #711 deleted the bar.) The remaining verbs stay the ratified per-unit priority walk — rescue/reload/rev are a lexical order, not a score — so the fallback list has **ATTACK removed**, or a member the joint pass deliberately declined would re-decide alone and undo it.

**One step of LOOKAHEAD** *(dev fork, pairs in v1, 2026-09-02)*: a candidate that scores nothing alone **and** puts a state on an enemy is priced by **the better of its own solo score and the best squadmate follow-up**, scored as the pair against the same base. Bounded by that trigger rather than a depth counter, so a squad of plain weapons pays nothing. The follow-up is not committed — the next round finds it on its own, against a plan that really holds the set-up.

**The "better of" is #711's correction, and it is the one thing that ticket would have shipped broken.** The accumulator floored at `Vector3i.ZERO` and the caller *replaced* the solo score with the result — invisible while a candidate had to beat zero, since it only ever turned a refusal into a refusal. With no bar it **launders a negative into a zero**: a set-up really worth `(-1, 0, -5)` came back `(0,0,0)` and then outranked an honest plain swing at `(-1, 8, -4)` on the first term, so a member facing a lethal counter would soak instead of hitting and die dealing nothing — the predictability contract broken by the ticket that invoked it. Capping at the caller is **not** the fix: the zero lives inside the accumulator, so the floor has to become the solo score itself.

**The scoring pass is not pure, and the ORDER of its restores is load-bearing.** `resolve_plan` publishes projections onto units, so the pass must restore the real plan **before it queues** as well as on the way out. `queue_action`'s whiff gate (`SquadPlanValidator.aim_finds_a_target`) reads published knockback and is correct *only* because that knockback belongs to the already-queued aims; a scoring pass publishes a candidate's shove too, so without the pre-queue restore the gate hunted for the target on a cell some rejected hypothetical had thrown it to, found nobody, and refused the winner as a whiff — **every attack with knockback was unqueueable by the joint pass.** Found by a mutant, invisible to any fixture whose weapon does not shove.

**THAT GATE IS NOT THE ONLY READER, and this section claimed it was until [#709](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/709) (2026-09-04).** `RulesService.gather_attack_victims` resolves occupants through `board.projected_unit_at_cell` — i.e. `get_projected_destination()`, published knockback included — and has since #105, so **the candidate BUILDER reads it too**. The restore-before-queue therefore fixed one reader and left the other: `_best_candidate_for` resolves a hypothetical per candidate with no restore between members, so the member iterated SECOND had its list built while the first member's last *rejected* hypothetical was still standing on the board. That is not a projected read at all — it is a read of a plan nobody gave, and its candidates could then be refused as whiffs against the restored board and have their keys poisoned in `refused` for the rest of the turn.

**So the ordering rule is now explicit: every member's candidates are built against the base plan BEFORE the first hypothetical** (`AITactics._candidates_by_member`, rebuilt per round and consumed by `_best_candidate_for` and `_lookahead` alike). The eligibility gate moved into that builder, so a missing entry IS the answer to "is this member still choosing" and the two readers cannot drift. Rebuilding per round is what keeps it as fresh as the base plan — nothing is committed within a round, so no entry can go stale before the single `queue_action`, and #706's property that the second member decides knowing what the first committed to is a CROSS-round one, carried by the next round's own base resolve.


**Dev rider (2026-07-22): this scoring rule is open to change as more AI kinds arrive.** It is deliberately one function (`AITactics._score_plan`) so a future archetype-flavored scorer swaps in one place.

Target-state awareness ships "minimal": lethality tiers (via the resolver's own prediction) + one builder tie-break — rescue the most urgent downed clock (intimidate's lowest-Will pick was the other, retired by #1174). Crisis and limb loss are seen by profile since #1230 (above, and the `limbs` term); deeper state reasoning -- Crisis exploitation, WHICH limb -- is future scoring-term work, and the seam for it is `_score_plan`.

## Weapon routines — a family's own say on WHEN its tool is worth using ([#726](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/726), 2026-09-03)

The integration contract above hands the AI every verb and attack for free; nothing in it says when a tool is worth **setting up** rather than firing. His framing on #117: *"we're going to need some weapon/ability specific routines the AI will have to be able to plug and play, depending on what tools are available to them."* `Classes/ai/weapons/AIWeaponRoutine.gd` is that seam: one class per `WeaponData.WeaponType`, dispatched the way `WeaponInstance._instance_for` is, with an **explicit entry for every family** — the inert base is a legal declaration, `MAIN_ACTION_NEVER`'s idiom — pinned by `tests/law/test_ai_weapon_routine_coverage.gd`, so a new family goes red until somebody declares its stance even when that stance is "nothing". A rune or an empty hand resolves to the base.

**A routine only ever says NO.** Two hooks, both inert on the base:

- `allows_preparation(unit, verb, board)` — asked by `queue_main_action` before a **weapon** verb's builder runs (`RELOAD`/`REV`/`BURROW`, the Weapon Action submenu's own set — never RESCUE, which is not a weapon's to veto). A refusal is the same shape as `can_reload()` answering false: a builder gate, not a skip of the walk.
- `defers_candidate(unit, candidate, plan, score)` — asked inside `_best_candidate_for` after the hypothetical is resolved and scored. **A deferred candidate loses to every candidate the same member did not defer and is still taken when it has nothing else** — deferred, never deleted, so #711 stays literal.

It never introduces a verb the archetype declared `NEVER`, never invents a target, never adds a score term. The archetype table stays the authority on *what* a squad will ever do; the routine decides *when* it is worth doing.

**A preparation is a RULE, not a SCORE** *(dev, 2026-09-03)*. `_score_plan` prices this turn; a preparation pays off next turn, which no term can see (the lookahead is one step *inside* the squad's own turn). The predictability contract *prefers* the legible rule — *"the spearman saves the spring for a line or a kill"* is a sentence a player can hold, where a discounted multi-turn expectation is the computation that law exists to reject. So `_score_plan` is untouched.

**Deferral is MEMBER-LOCAL, and that is the answer to the obvious objection.** #720 deleted a two-tier structure a day earlier in favour of one ranking — but that tier duplicated arithmetic the score already did (a body prices at +1 unaided), where this one expresses a cost the score has *no* term for. It is applied inside `_best_candidate_for` only; `_Scored` and the joint loop are untouched, because threading a flag through them would make it a precedence *across members* — the shape #720 deleted — and let one family's routine reorder another family's swing. Pinned by `test_deferral_is_member_local_never_a_precedence_across_members`. Declared limit: a target only the deferred attack can reach still gets it, because the alternative is not attacking.

**The rules, and the sentence each one is:**

| Family | Rule | The sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Drill** | `BURROW` only when the **projected destination** has no COVER (`board.cover_def_at`) | *A drill with nothing to hit digs in where it stands — once.* |
| **Springspear** | defer an attack that **disarms** (spends readiness with no other selectable attack that fires without it) unless its own volley catches `SPRING_LINE_MINIMUM` enemies or its marginal removal term is positive | *The spearman saves the spring for a line or a kill.* |

- The Drill's cell is the projected destination because the deposit lands there (`SquadManager.resolve_plan`, pinned by `test_cover_lands_where_the_digger_ends_up`) and the fallback walk runs after the group move is queued; `movement.cell` would be Law #4's exact drift. `can_burrow()` is unconditionally true, so without the rule an idle drill re-digs its own cover every turn — a no-op deposit (`TerrainStateManager.fold` dedupes), an order and an `AI_PLAN_READ` beat spent on nothing.
- The Springspear's "disarms" is read off the content, not assumed of the family: `Stab` requires readiness without consuming it, so a spent spring gates every attack and the next turn is a Spring Load (and a sprung spear cannot counter either — `attack_source_can_counter` reads the same gate). Over two turns Stab-Stab beats Spring-then-reload unless the line catches two or the +2 fells. "Fells" is the marginal removal term (`score.removals`; a mission point counts too), never `_plan_removes` on the hypothetical, which reads true when a squadmate already felled the target. `SPRING_LINE_MINIMUM` is the one tunable, a `# playtest-tunable` static on the routine (the `FallRules`/`Abilities` idiom) — not GameKnobs, which addresses node properties in the running Battle3D world.

**Two families that LOOK like they want a routine and declare the bare base, recorded so nobody re-derives them:**

- **Chainsword** — a proximity rule (*"rev when an enemy is within a move"*) was proposed and killed in review. `REV` is last in every list, so refusing it means the unit *idles* for a free refresh; a "not already revved" clause costs a refresh on the tick cadence (rev at N, refused at N+2, next swing lands unrevved); and *"a chainsword with nothing to hit revs"* is the simpler sentence. **Rev stays unconditional and stays a fallback — no ATTACK preemption** *(dev, 2026-09-03: "rev only when it can't attack", declined rather than deferred)*. What changed is only the table: Hold/Sentry may rev now.
  - **RE-AFFIRMED 2026-09-16 against [#931](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/931), which asked to repeal it.** That ticket read the every-turn refresh as a *watchability* problem — a main action burned on a no-op, too dull to watch — and proposed the "not already revved" clause as the cure. The cost it was weighed against was camera time, and camera time is what got fixed: **an AI's Rev now takes no beat at all** (`Pacing.CODA_EARNS_AN_AI_BEAT`; see [visual-clarity.md](visual-clarity.md)), so the loop costs a queue row and nothing else. Dev's call: *"this doesn't mean we have to change chainsword mechanics, we can change camera mechanics too."* **The rule above stands untouched, and so does its reasoning** — what is gone is the argument that was being made against it. A future case for gating Rev has to be made on its own gameplay merits: whether a chainsword should be able to hold the DEF-pierce permanently for the price of a main action it had nothing else to spend.
- **Kinetic Mace** — the economy is already priced. Blowback and Smash are both `power 3` Manhattan-1, but they do not tie against anything that can counter: `_resolve_actions` publishes each aim's shove *before* counters derive and `can_counter` reads the defender's **projected** cell, so a Blowback that shoves a Manhattan-1 defender off the attacker **denies its counter** and wins on the reaction term; near a drop or water it wins on damage outright. The mace alternates Smash/Blowback against melee on its own, and a "defer Blowback unless it beats Smash" rule is exactly what the argmax already does — a mutant deleting it would survive every test. (#117's *"mace charge → Blowback has no scoring"* was true until #706 derived counters from post-shove positions; it is stale now and ticked there.)

## Known v1 approximations (accepted at ratification)

- **Destination planning reads the default pick** — `best_attack_destination` hoists one `leader.get_fired_attack()` and evaluates every cell against it, rather than per-candidate-attack. Cells × attacks × enemies was judged not worth it yet.
  - **A null pick reads as adjacency here, on purpose (#1215).** A leader with nothing selectable still plans to stand within melee of its target, because `Reach` treats a null attack as Manhattan 1. That is what keeps a dry leader marching its armed squad into the fight; reading it as "no reach" would stall them. The cost was that a dry SOLO unit walked up to an enemy and then had nothing to do, **fixed by #1220** (ruling 5): a dry unit leading nobody armed falls back out of reach instead (*Members who find nothing*).
  - **Narrowed by [#760](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/760) (2026-10-03):** the seek that runs after it searches every selectable attack from every reachable cell, but only for a removal or a squad break (*Seeking a removal or a squad break* above). For everything else the default pick still decides where the leader stands.
  - Corrected 2026-08-06 by [#127](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/127), which is worth knowing because the failure was *silent and permanent*: the approach ranked candidate cells by hops toward **the enemy's own square**, and `path_hops` is deliberately occupancy-blind, so a downed body parked on the one adjacent firing cell in a corridor read as the shortest way in. The unit walked up to the corpse and stopped — every turn, forever, because a dead end is stable. Two halves were both required, and each was falsified alone: the route now targets the nearest **standable** firing cell (`_nearest_standable_attack_cell`), *and* the ranking walk opts into occupancy (`path_hops(…, block_on_occupancy = true)`) so it stops imagining it can cut straight through the bodies in the way. Retargeting alone still stalled. The generalizable shape: **when a metric and the thing it measures disagree about what is passable, fixing the target does not fix the measure.** Note the two halves have **different scopes**, which is the part worth copying: the retarget is attack-specific (a firing position is not the target's own square), but the honest metric is not — it is unconditional in `_best_approach`, so **Sentry's walk home got the same fix**. Scoping it to the attack case would have meant gating it on the retarget parameter, i.e. one flag answering two unrelated questions, and `closest_reachable_cell_to` could never have reached it. A sentry stalls identically against an enemy holding a corridor; pinned by `test_walking_home_routes_around_a_body_holding_the_corridor`.
  - **Target selection was then brought onto the same metric (same day, same sweep).** Fixing the approach alone left `nearest_enemy` measuring occupancy-*blind* while the approach measured aware — two answers to "how far is that enemy", so the AI could pick a target it would only then discover was the long way round. Both now measure to a **standable firing cell** rather than the target's own square, via one shared `_standable_attack_cells`. Note the detail that makes it possible at all: an active enemy blocks passage, so an occupancy-aware walk *to enemy squares* scores every enemy UNREACHABLE — routing to firing cells is what lets target selection use the honest metric. Still one BFS for all enemies (the `until` set is the union). Pinned by `test_nearest_enemy_measures_to_a_firing_position_not_to_the_target_itself`, and **the whole rest of the suite passes with the old metric restored** — which is why this went unnoticed.
- ~~**Counters aren't scored**~~ and ~~**Squad-level coordination**~~ — **both closed 2026-09-02 by the plan-marginal rebuild above.** What they leave behind is three narrower approximations, each named so nobody re-derives it:
  - **The opener bears the counter alone — no longer a freeze, and no longer anything at all.** C1/C4 make a defending party react once per plan, to the *first* attack against it, so the whole cost lands on whichever candidate is scored first. Until #711 that froze a squad outright: if those counters would fell the opener, no candidate netted a positive removal and it would not open even when the follow-ups won the exchange (playtest symptom, *"the enemy won't attack my tank"*). With no bar the squad opens. **And nothing clever replaces it, which is worth stating so nobody goes looking**: `SquadManager.choose_counter_target` picks the first legal member of the attacking *party* in member order (or a taunter) and never asks who opened, and in round 1 nobody has moved — so every candidate aimed at a given defending squad draws the identical volley, the cost is constant across the argmax, and it cancels. Round 1 is decided by removals and damage dealt as though counters did not exist.
  - ~~**Reaction heals are invisible.**~~ **Moot since 2026-09-28** — the reactive heal (C8–C10) was repealed by [#1135](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1135), so `plan.counters` carries counters only and there is no unscored heal left to undo the AI's chip damage.
  - ~~**Heals and end-of-turn tile damage are not terms.**~~ **Heals ARE terms since #1220** (rulings 3 and 16, above). End-of-turn tile damage is still not one: an attack candidate never changes where anyone ends the turn, so the DESTINATION rule carries it instead (no cell ended on a hazard while a safe one offers the same).
  - **Lingering deposits are not priced** (#1220). A map-only attack's aim is kept for what it does this pass; ground it leaves burning or soaked for a later turn earns nothing. On #117.
  - **A squadmate's queued soak does not open an arc** (#1220). Candidates read wetness live, as the whiff gate does, so the combo where one member soaks a target and the next shocks it is invisible to the builder. On #117.
  - **A deferred candidate can still price a pair** (#726). `_lookahead` builds follow-ups straight from `_attack_candidates`, so a set-up may be credited with a deferred Spring the squad will not choose next round. Inert for shipped content (no deferred candidate applies a state); the fix would be a delete rather than a defer, so it stays a declared limit on #117.
  - **Reload-vs-poke** (#117's third instance, deferred by #726 rather than dropped). A unit carrying a dry weapon *and* a second fireable non-readiness attack pokes forever, since ATTACK always yields a candidate and RELOAD sits behind it in the walk. Unreachable with shipped content — no family carries that pair — and the Carbine's routine is where it would land.
- **The MOVEMENT layer still measures to LIVE enemy cells** — `_engageable_enemies`, `nearest_enemy`, `_approach_distances` and `best_attack_destination` all read `enemy.movement.cell`, where the attack pass now reads the plan's. **Declared rather than fixed, and #709 deliberately left it there**: the two cannot disagree in a real turn, because the movement layer runs before this squad queues anything and, since the hand-off drain, every other faction's queue is empty at that moment. The case it WOULD serve is [#710](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/710)'s preview, which runs the AI while the player's plan is pending — and swapping these six reads does **not** get the preview there, which is the finding worth carrying: `aim_finds_a_target` positions a foreign unit with the ACTING squad's action list, so a unit with no order in that list falls through to its live cell and an aim built at its projected one is refused as a whiff; and every `_resolve_actions` opens by clearing all published knockback, so the player's own shoves are gone the moment the AI resolves anything. The preview needs the gate reworked or a snapshot of the player's plan, not a read swap. (#710 landed WITHOUT touching any of these: its hover tier reads LIVE cells by design, and its plan tier stands every unit on its projected cell for the duration instead, so the eight reads here see the post-plan board without being edited. They stay declared, and the argument for leaving them is unchanged.)
  - **The argument covers the MOVEMENT layer only, and `nearest_enemy` had a second caller it did not cover ([#1209](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1209), 2026-10-04).** The watch builder asks it too, from the fallback walk, which runs AFTER the squad's attacks are queued. There, "the movement layer runs before this squad queues anything" is false. A squadmate's queued blow could fell the enemy the watch faced, or a queued shove could empty the cell it faced, and the Guard builder's exposure count had the same flaw.
    - **Both preparation builders now decide on the board the squad's plan leaves.** Each one:
      1. resolves the plan so far;
      2. stands every unit on its planned cell with `AIController.stand_on_projected` (#710's snapshot, so every live-cell read inside agrees at once);
      3. reads lifecycle through the plan's hypo;
      4. puts every unit back before queueing.
    - `nearest_enemy` and `_exposure_counts` gained an optional hypo. Empty means the live board, which is what pursuit still passes.
- ~~**Movement never seeks rescue targets**~~ — **the rescue walk since #1220** (ruling 12): a member with nobody to hit from any follow cell walks beside the most urgent body when its archetype rescues. A LEADER is never moved to rescue.
- **Movement is not scored at all** — joint selection prices *what a member does*, never *where it stands*. `score(plan with X at cell C)` is the same delta and is the natural next step, but the destination pick is still the group move below.
  - **Narrowed by #760:** where a unit stands IS scored now, by standing it on each cell, but only for a removal or a squad break. Damage alone moves nobody, by ruling.
  - **Narrowed again by #1220 (ruling 9):** damage moves a MEMBER inside the leash, judged by geometry rather than a resolve -- *can it hit anyone from there*. The leader is still moved only by a removal, a break or the mission.
- **The danger field is an UPPER BOUND** (#1220), so the dry fall-back's *fewest enemies reach it* and Balanced's safety tie-break are approximate: the field assumes every enemy squad charges.
- **Routes are asked of the LIVE watches** (#920). A watch a squadmate's earlier walk in the same pass would spend still counts, so the route errs toward the detour.
- **Mixed bands in one squad's joint pass** (#1230). Each member's candidates are scored under its own profile and the round takes the best across members, so a sloppy member can win a round with a score a disciplined one would not give.
- **The threat preview plans squads as they stand** (#1230). A regroup at the enemy's turn start is not in it.
- **A stray's memory of its old squad is not saved** (#1230). After a load -- a resume mid-enemy-turn included -- it joins the nearest squad instead.
- **Setting a band in the Unit Editor demotes a cast-reference unit to a snapshot**, as Must survive does (#259). Enemies are embedded, so they are unaffected.
- **Movement is a group move, not per-unit destinations** — both moving archetypes call `queue_group_move`, so a member's destination is "preserve your path-offset from the leader" rather than anything tactical. Measured 2026-07-29 while fixing [#103](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/103): this is **not** why the AI authored illegal plans (a single individual leader move produces the identical refusal — the invalid order is the hold-position filler every member gets (from `SquadManager` itself since #46; a `game.gd` handler before that, which is why headless boards never grew it), and the binding rule is `SquadPlanValidator._check_leader_range`, which applies to any plan from any author). But `GroupMoveSolver` is currently the AI's *only* cohesion solver, so this cannot simply be deleted. The replacement needs no new solver — `RulesService.compute_move_range` already leashes a non-leader to the leader's *projected* destination, so queueing the leader first makes each member's own range cohesion-clamped — what it needs is a decision about each archetype's per-unit movement taste. Tracked on [#117](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/117).
  - **Narrowed by #760:** a member the seek assigns a removal or a break is PINNED to its cell (`GroupMoveSolver.plan(…, pinned)`), and the formation places everyone else around it. Everyone else still follows the offset rule.

**The standing home for all of the above is [#117](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/117)** (evergreen), added 2026-07-29 on the premise that the AI is permanently behind the feature set: every system we add creates AI work that lands after the system ships. New approximations go there as well as here — here for the doctrine, there for the queue.

## Target selection — TWO SYSTEMS, forked on "is anybody attackable this turn?"

*(Dev ruling, 2026-09-02, from playtest: an enemy adjacent to a spearman that could counter, with a mage one step beyond that could not, took the spearman. Both were attackable that turn.)*

- **Somebody is → the best EXCHANGE wins, and distance is only the tie-break.** His words: *"the closest possible unit isn't what should be picked, but the best possible trade for the attacker… absolute distance was not even, but that should not matter since both attacks were in range that turn."*
- **Nobody is → pursue the NEAREST.** `nearest_enemy` is the pursuit half alone.
- **A BODY COUNTS AS ATTACKABLE, and ranks below the standing in both halves** ([#720](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/720), dev 2026-09-03). Both selectors used to answer only about the standing, so a squad with a body in reach and nothing else read the fork as *nobody* and fell into pursuit — which then preferred **any** standing enemy anywhere, including three archers behind a wall it had no route to. Every candidate cell scored `UNREACHABLE`, the straight-line fallback answered with the cell the squad was already on, and five attackers spent thirteen rounds of Castle Assault revving two cells from a downed general who was holding the gate shut. Killing him was the whole game: the gate cell is standable once he is gone.
  - **Engagement ranks `still standing > cannot answer me > fewer hops`.** Standing sits **above safety**, and that ordering is the whole of what makes this a ranking rather than a reversal: a body can never counter, so it is unconditionally *safe* and would win every comparison it entered.
  - **Pursuit ranks `fewer hops > still standing > straight-line distance`.** Two walks (every active enemy, then bodies only if that found nobody) collapsed into one, because two walks *are* absolute precedence — which is what walked the squad past a body at its feet. Standing sits **below route** here for the mirror-image reason: above it, the old bug is back.
  - **The layers agree about bodies almost everywhere**, so the change is mostly a Law #4 one — both rank by route now, and excluding bodies from engagement merely sends the question to pursuit, which usually lands on the same target. `allowed` is the one thing only engagement reads, which is why the case that pins the fork is a **leashed** one; a mutant found that, after the obvious fixture passed against it.


**Pursuit stays nearest deliberately, and the reasons are design rather than cost.** It is Rushdown's *identity* — a rusher that hunts the softest target across the board is the **Balanced** archetype wearing the wrong name. It rewards the player for screening a mage behind a frontline, which is the same thing #57's rescue window rewards. And it is *legible*: "it goes for whoever is closest" is a rule you can bait and funnel, where "it goes for its best target" is a computation the player cannot see (Law #1's spirit, applied to the AI's reasoning).

`AITactics.choose_engagement_target` is the fork; `RushdownArchetype` and `SentryArchetype` both call it. **Sentry passes BOTH its leashes and they answer different questions:** `within` tests the *enemy's own cell* (is this intruder in my zone), `allowed` tests the *cell I would fight from*. Dropping either lures a sentry out — one by target, one by footing.

**The exchange term is a BOOLEAN — can this target answer me from the cell I would attack from — not a scored one**. A hypothetical MOVE moves nobody, so this layer cannot resolve the fight it is choosing. Scoring a cell IS possible by standing the unit on it, which is what the seek does (#760, above), but only for a removal or a squad break, by ruling. So who to fight stays this boolean. It is asked through `SquadManager.can_counter`, which takes the attacker's cell as a parameter rather than `AITactics` re-deriving counter reach — Law #4's own words, *"if you cannot reach the existing answer from where you are standing, take it as a parameter"*. A private counter-reach predicate would be exactly the drift #78 exists to stop.

**One BFS, and the legality filter runs BEFORE it.** `_engageable_enemies` collects each enemy's firing cells that are both in move range and inside `allowed`, then walks `path_hops` once over the union (`_approach_distances`' own trick). Asking for the globally nearest firing cell and testing *that* is wrong twice: it excludes an enemy whose nearest cell is outside the leash when another one inside it would serve, and it pays a whole BFS per enemy — measured at +21% on Castle Assault before the fix, and *faster than baseline* after it.

**Declared asymmetry:** this layer judges an exchange by a boolean, the attack pick (`_score_plan`) by graded resolver damage. Justified rather than sloppy — this layer cannot resolve at all, and the attack pick can and would be throwing information away. Stated here so the pair is a design rather than a drift.

**Known limits, declared:**

- ~~**The engageable set is OPTIMISTIC for a leader with squadmates.**~~ **Closed by #1220.** It used the leader's own unclamped move range, so cohesion could refuse the group move to a cell the leader alone could stand on, and in play that read as *"it went for the mage and then didn't."* `_engageable_enemies`, `best_attack_destination` and the seek's leader cells now ask only cells the squad can FOLLOW it to (`GroupMoveSolver.followable_destinations`, the sweep the player's Move overlay paints grey with, #1069), computed once per squad decision and shared (`_followable`).
- **Leader-only.** `engage` group-moves the whole squad toward the leader's pick, so a target good for the leader may be poor for a member. Widening it is #117's per-unit movement item. **Narrowed by #760:** a member that can take a removal or a squad break is pinned to the cell it takes it from, so the leader's pick no longer decides that member's footing. **And by #1220:** a member with nobody to hit steps to a follow cell it can hit from.

## Balanced ([#1220](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1220), 2026-10-05; rulings 11, 13, 14 and 18 on #117)

The archetype that weighs a fight before walking into it -- the answer Rushdown deliberately does not give. `BalancedArchetype.take_squad_turn` picks with `AITactics.choose_balanced_target` and then rides the shared `engage` with `weigh_safety`.

- **With anybody in reach, the best exchange**, exactly Rushdown's pick (`_best_exchange`, now shared by both).
- **With nobody, the nearest enemy that could NOT answer it** from the cell it would fight from (`SquadManager.can_counter`, the boolean the engagement layer already asks), ranked as pursuit ranks; only when every enemy could answer, the nearest at all. `_approach_firing` keeps the firing cell `_approach_distances` already found, so this costs no second walk.
- **Re-picked every turn.** Nothing is remembered between turns, which is what keeps it legible.
- **The one archetype that weighs SAFETY** (ruling 11): among cells it could attack from, the one fewest enemies could reach next turn wins, before the route does; among cells it cannot, safety is the last tie-break before distance. Read off `ThreatField.build` from its own side.
- **Hold's and Sentry's whole action list** (ruling 18), so it rescues, watches and guards.

**Declared:** *"cannot answer me"* is judged at the NEAREST firing cell, and the approach may then stand on a different one. And the field it reads safety off is an upper bound (above).

## Regrouping ([#1230](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/1230), 2026-10-05)

Until #1230 an enemy's squads only ever shrank: a member downed, left out of its leader's range at a pass's end, or overflowed at a leader swap became a squad of one, kept its archetype (#1196), and never rejoined. The dev's rulings, all Medium and up:

- **Old squad first, else the nearest with room** -- `AITactics.regroup_target`, the one rule both halves below ask. The old squad is `Unit.left_squad`, stamped by `SquadManager.eject`; battle-scoped, never saved. Nearest is by route to each leader. Candidates come from the LIVE squad list, which a squad leaves the moment its last member does (`destroy_empty_squad` erases before it frees), so a stray remembering a dying squad is never offered it.
- **Same archetype only, as a toggle** (*"maybe we'll want hard enemies to be more flexible"*): `joins_any_archetype`, off in all three bands. Archetypes compare through `AIArchetype.effective`, the one spelling of FACTION_DEFAULT.
- **Strays may form a new squad together** (*"only medium+, and has to abide by previous rulings"*): joining a lone unit's squad needs `squads_up` on both, and the higher leadership leads (`successor_among`'s rule, board order on a tie), so two strays never pick each other.
- **The join** is `AIController.regroup`, at the head of BOTH walks -- `take_faction_turn` and the Play API's `_take_ai_turn` -- after the hand-off reset, before any squad plans. It joins in range only, through the player's own door (`join_squad`, gated by `can_squad_up`), and repeats until a pass joins nobody. An AI join plays the tether draw-in and is recorded as a `join`, which the replay needs.
- **The walk back, only when idle:** `AITactics.regroup_walk`, asked by Rushdown and Balanced in place of pursuit when the stray has nobody it can fight this turn; it walks toward the squad it would join, then takes its fallback actions. A stray that can attack still attacks. Rushdown's defended-point walk still comes first, Sentry already walks home, and Hold never moves.

## Not this layer

Strain's fate (#76 — its AI integration is already free by construction), the ability-chassis content itself (#61, closed).

**Balanced is BUILT** (above). Its definition dates from 2026-09-02, out of the target-selection ruling -- *pick the best target from the start and take what you meet on the way* -- and that is why the two poles stay far apart: smear target quality into Rushdown and there is nothing left for Balanced to be.

**Win/loss detection is no longer a leftover** — it landed 2026-07-28 as #96 and lives outside this layer, in `MissionRules`/`MissionController` ([missions.md](missions.md)). The AI has two points of contact:

- **A guard.** `AIController.take_faction_turn` stops issuing orders the moment the mission is over, so a squad that wipes the player mid-turn doesn't keep playing behind the end-of-mission card.
- **`CAPTURE` is `MAIN_ACTION_NEVER` on all four archetypes** (Balanced joined with #1220), forced to be an explicit decision by `tests/law/test_ai_action_coverage.gd`'s partition. **This one is not drift.** Rev/Burrow *were* `NEVER` for want of a builder, until 2026-08-06 and #726 respectively (and RALLY sat `NEVER` as a parked knob until #1174 retired it); `CAPTURE` is `NEVER` because there is nothing for an AI faction to *win* by capturing — enemy objectives are out of #96's scope, and the point belongs to the player. The AI contests it positionally with what it already has: Rushdown walks into the approach, and a Sentry squad zoned over the point defends it with no AI code at all. **[#571](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/571) *defend a point* shipped 2026-09-11 and did NOT need one** — this line used to name it as "the first thing that will need a real capture builder", and it needed no builder at all. The dev's rule is PRESENCE: a hostile standing in a `Kind.DEFEND` zone loses the mission outright, so there is no action to build, only a **destination**. `RushdownArchetype` walks at the cargo (Rushdown only, dev's call) and then takes its ordinary main actions; `MAIN_ACTION_PRIORITY` and `MAIN_ACTION_NEVER` are untouched. **The general lesson is the one #751 already paid for twice: check whether a filed item is free before building it — and check what the RULE is before assuming which layer implements it.**
