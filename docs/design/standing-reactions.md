**Why these exist (the throughline, dev 2026-08-19):** push the player from *"does my queued plan work"* toward genuinely predicting what the ENEMY PHASE will do — more of the puzzle living in anticipating reactive/triggered events, not just resolving an already-known queue. A reactive defense (Guard) and a reactive attack (Overwatch) are the two halves of one idea, which is why they share a grammar and a doc. ## The shared grammar

One lifetime rule for every standing reaction, teachable as a sentence: **a standing reaction arms when declared, absorbs exactly one trigger, and otherwise lapses when its owner's next turn begins.**

- **Declared as a main action; arms at its slot in the resolution order.** Not at end of pass — in-sequence arming is what lets queue order sequence a same-pass combo (arm the watch, then shove them into it) or dodge a self-trigger (fire your splashy AoE *before* your Guard arms). R6's order-is-the-lever doctrine, paying rent.
- **Absorbs exactly one trigger, then spent.**
- **Untriggered, it lapses when the owner's faction's next turn begins** — "the enemy phase ends" generalized to N factions (TurnManager cycles any number of teams); it covers every hostile faction's phase in between. Consequences: no fire-and-forget turret builds (a watch that outlives its owner's attention is a different mechanic — a mine — and a deliberate non-goal), and during your own pass the only live standing reactions are ones armed *this* pass.
- **The anchor rule.** Overwatch: the owner leaving its anchor cell drops the watch, however that happens (shove, rescue carry, lifecycle) — the footprint is frozen geometry aimed from that cell. Guard: the *pair* separating beyond the Guard's range drops it (see Guard below — deliberately range-shaped, not cell-shaped).

**The listening grammar** — three verses, one per reaction kind, and they never collide because each listens to a different thing:

> **Counters listen to authored attacks. Watches listen to entries. Guards listen to hits on their ward.**

Corollaries, each load-bearing:
- **A derived attack is never counter-bait.** A triggered overwatch shot draws no counter (its victim's retaliation is the rest of their own turn); a counter draws no counter — one derivation level, ever, matching [squad-system.md](squad-system.md) C1–C10 untouched.
- **Watch-to-watch chains are legal.** A watch's shot with knockback throwing the crosser into a second watch triggers it — that is the entry rule applied honestly, not a counter chain. Deterministic overwatch pinball, fully previewed; every link is authored content (knockback on an overwatch-capable attack), so degenerate chains get fixed in content, not rules.
- **A substituted hit is never Guard-bait** — no substitution chains. **ON HOLD, not buried** (dev, 2026-08-20): there may be silly edge cases where a tank conga is *wanted*; the door stays open, nothing ships through it yet.

## Queue order becomes real ([#412](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/412)) — **BUILT 2026-08-26**

The structural fork the Overwatch scratchpad entry left open, resolved: **cut (b)**. Move orders resolve in queued order — the order they sit in the queue is the order the resolver walks them — and the player may **drag-reorder** queued moves. Units are still *shown* moving together; simultaneity survives as presentation — with one beat in it since [#567](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/567): a crosser halts on its crossing cell while its own triggered shot plays, and its siblings keep walking through it. This extends **R6** ([resolution-pipeline.md](resolution-pipeline.md)): attacks already resolve sequentially in queued order as the declared skill expression, and moves now join them. The queue is the clock for every mid-move event — a watch trigger is the first customer; future on-entry mechanics (traps, fire-on-entry) inherit the same clock.

What deliberately does not change: **whole-plan validation** (destination conflicts and formation rotations are order-independent questions — two squadmates swapping cells stays one legal simultaneous fact; transient ally co-occupancy during ordered playback is the rotation fiction, already legal since allies never block); **Law #2 machinery** (a reorder is a plan edit riding the synchronous resolve→repaint chain); **Group Move as one authoring gesture** (the solver's member order becomes the *default* row order, individually reorderable after — member order is now mechanically real, no longer cosmetics).

The rejected cuts, for the record: **(a)** spatial precedence ("nearest crosser along the line eats it") was a proxy for time that could contradict the watched story and didn't generalize to tile watches; **(c)** lockstep walk-time (earliest path-step entering the footprint eats the shot) was offered and declined — the dev wants explicit order as the mechanic, the bigger design space. If (b) ever needs revisiting, whatever replaces it must *replace* the clock, never sit beside it as an override (Law #4).

### How it was built (2026-08-26) — and what turned out already to exist

**The clock and the lever were both mostly already there, and re-deriving from the code before planning is what found that** (the issue's own build map predates #414). `SquadActionQueueControl` had the whole drag — press, live re-parent, release, emit — gated to attack rows alone; and `SquadManager.resolve_plan`'s walk over `squad.action_queue` was already the pass's clock, which is exactly what #414's Guard arms on. What the ticket actually needed was to open the gate and to make moves *visible* to that walk.

- **ONE reorder seam, not one per section.** `Squad.reorder_attacks_by_actor` generalized to `reorder_by_actor(type, actors)` — "by actor" holds for every reorderable type, since a unit gets one move and one main action — and **which rows may be dragged is the ORDER's own answer** (`BaseAction.is_reorderable`), not the panel's. A derived counter says no, a hold-position filler says no, everything else says yes. Consequence worth naming: **side-channel rows became draggable too, and stacked-Guard precedence was already real and unreachable** — `PlanResolver._guard_for` takes the first match in `plan.guards`, so row order had always decided who absorbs, with no way for the player to touch it.
- **The move phase is a BLOCK ahead of the attack walk, not moves interleaved into it.** The panel's sections, `OrderExecutor`'s phases and this walk are one order, stated once; cross-type ordering is not displayable in a sectioned panel, is not draggable, and would break Law #2 against a parallel move playback. "Moves resolve in queued order" therefore means *within the move block*, which is also what #413's annotation-on-the-crossing-move's-row assumes.
- **Seeding every mover at its ORIGIN before any of them walks IS the ticket.** `PlanResolver._hypo_for` seeds a unit at its projected DESTINATION — right everywhere except inside the move phase itself. With the seed, a shot fired while mover #1 crosses gathers its victims with movers #2 and #3 still standing where they started; without it the walk is a list order with no consequence. It threads through `projected_position(unit, hypo)`, the incumbent seam, never a second projection.
- **The regression guarantee, and it is pinned:** when the block ends, every uninterrupted mover is back at its destination, so the attack walk, the counters, the cell effects and the board preview are bit-for-bit what they were. The halted crosser is the one deliberate exception.

## Overwatch ([#413](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/413)) — **BUILT 2026-08-26**

**A queued attack that doesn't fire on declaration — it stands watch and fires when an enemy enters the watched cells during another faction's phase.** Not a surprise, ever: the footprint is telegraphed on the board, and a queued move that would cross a standing watch shows the shot it triggers in the queue immediately.

- **The watch is a pre-aimed shot.** Declaring = aiming an attack through the **normal targeting flow** — same menu, same facing pick, same geometry. **The overlay is NOT the same, and this clause read as a rule saying it was until [#591](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/591) made it false (2026-08-27):** what the sameness was protecting is the *choice* — aiming is aiming, and "tile or line" must not become a second decision — never the *picture*, and identical pictures left the player unable to tell which verb they were committing except by remembering which ring row they clicked. A watch aim now paints its own reach wash and its own footprint fill (`OverlayManager.WATCH_REACH_MODULATE`/`WATCH_HOVER_MODULATE`, both Game-tab knobs). The watched footprint = that aim's affected cells, frozen at declare time with the attack itself (the `fired_attack` stamp pattern, Law #2 provenance). *"Tile or line" is not a new choice*: a directional pattern watches its whole spread for the chosen facing, a point-targeted attack watches the aimed cell — the existing targeting-mode split, verbatim. The sniper fantasy ([weapons.md](weapons.md): "PER-flavored overwatch on a sniper-type carbine") is just a longer authored pattern. A watch cannot reach cells the attack couldn't hit — deliberate; a trap divorced from weapon reach is a different mechanic.
- **Capability is an authored flag on the `AttackData` BASE** — so a carbine's `WeaponAttackData` and a transmutation carving carry it through one mechanism, like `heals` and `deals_no_damage` before it. Whether base carbines have it stock or a mod grants it (Watchman's Sear's original job) is a content call at build time; the flag authors both.
- **Fires once, then spent, spending one magazine round.** Watchman's Sear's own "first enemy entering the line takes the shot," now canon. Fires-at-every-crosser is future keystone-mod space ("suppressing fire"), not v1 — one shot is what makes *who crosses first* the whole game, bounds the ordering problem to one event, and rhymes with Guard's one-hit consumption.
- **Trigger: an ACTIVE enemy of the watcher enters any watched cell, however it got there.** Walking or displacement — **shove combos are intended design** (mace them into your squadmate's line; queue order decides whether the combo connects, since the watch arms at its slot). Riders, each deliberate: allies never trigger a watch, but a triggered shot **is** the attack, full stop — ally splash lands if the attack has it, previewed, your fault; arming does not fire on units already standing in the footprint (the trigger is *entry*); moving between two watched cells is an entry (no safe repositioning inside the line), stepping out is not; **downed bodies don't trigger** ("probably" — held open pending AI self-preservation exploration; the accepted cut is that you can't spend a watch by throwing a corpse through it); **non-movement entries don't trigger in v1** — rescue placement, future spawns — with one recorded future exception the dev wants for comedy: *a rescue delivering its patient into the line of fire, triggering the shot*.
- **Basic shots don't interrupt the move.** Damage lands at the crossing moment (the crosser's later actions resolve at post-shot HP, and a second watch down the same path only triggers if they're still standing); the crosser continues to its destination. A crosser the shot **downs** stops at the crossing cell — lifecycle, not a new rule, and `LethalityRules.predict()` already answers it in preview. **Stopping power is an authored per-attack flag** beside the readiness flags (default off; mods grant it; whether it should be *default* is a playtest question answered by flipping content, never code — dev, 2026-08-20). **Involuntary displacement ends a move** — you can't resume a path you were thrown off, so a knockback overwatch shot is stopping power in disguise. What a halted-but-alive unit's remaining queued orders do is **parked with the stopping-shot content pass** — basic overwatch never raises it.
- **Queue representation: the triggered shot is its own ROW, indented under the crossing move** — the same shape an expanded volley's hits already use, and drawn by the same `_make_row(list, action, 1, false)`. A **deliberate divergence** from how counters display (they have their own section): chosen because #412's whole payoff is dragging a move row and watching who eats the shot change — the feedback must live with the row being dragged. The watcher's own declaration is an ordinary main-action row. **Settled by [#592](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/592) (2026-08-27), which found it had never been visible at all**: #413 shipped it as TEXT appended to `ActionQueueRow`'s `description_label`, which has been `visible = false` since the panel's first version because the row is icon-only by design — so every hop was correct and the last one wrote where nobody could see it, for the mechanic's whole life. `report.md` projects the ENTRIES rather than the label, which is why a bug report showed the note while the screen never did. A first fix gave the text its own visible label and the dev refused it on sight — *"those colors clash… I'd like them to mirror the general action queue setup by just being another action queue row with the firing unit, the weapon icon, and the unit getting hit"* — which is both better looking and less code: a watch shot IS an `AttackAction`, so it already answers every question a row asks (actor sprite, action icon with its lethality rung, target sprite) and inherits the panel's own styling, leaving nothing to tune. It is **not draggable and carries no X** (`AttackAction.is_reorderable` is false for a watch shot): it is derived, not an order anybody gave, and it is not in `action_queue` at all. A shot that SPLASHES draws one row per victim, exactly as an expanded volley does.
- **Board display: always visible, both directions** — your watches to the enemy, theirs to you (Axiom 4's telegraph; the *victim* stays undirected). Its own visual vocabulary: crosshair/flash-shaped threat marks, **not** a filled-square range layer (dev: "they shouldn't just be filled in squares"). Exact look is build-time work behind knobs. **Parity is mandatory** — this is gameplay information; it reaches both the 2D and 3D views, no [#292](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/292) asymmetry. **ONE mark, THREE states as of #591** — aiming (the colour fork above), QUEUED, and armed. The queued one is the same mark GHOSTED, at `_ghosted`'s alpha, which is the ignite preview's and therefore already the board's word for *planned, not yet real*; that is [#450](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/450) part 2's answer taken verbatim, and for its reason — every other queued verb previews, and until #591 a declared watch was visible only as a queue row until you pressed Execute. A cell an ARMED watch already marks is skipped rather than stacked, so a standing threat outranks a promised one. The ghost rides the armed mark's own plane in both views (`Layer.WATCH_ICONS` in 3D) — it is the same mark saying "not yet", and a channel of its own would be a second plane for one question.

### How it was built (#413, 2026-08-26) — and the rulings the build had to make

**WATCHES LISTEN TO ENTRIES, and there are exactly TWO moments an entry happens: a step of a queued walk, and the landing of a shove.** One rule, one implementation (`PlanResolver.fire_watch_entries`), which is what makes the shove combo and the watch-to-watch chain the *same mechanism* rather than two features that have to be kept in step. A shot's knockback moving anybody re-enters them wherever they landed, so the pinball falls out of the entry rule instead of being written down separately.

**The shot is a DERIVED `AttackAction` resolved through `resolve_attack_group`** — #414's lesson applied a second time. DEF mitigation, elemental immunity, Iron Will, the knockback direction and landing, the elevation stamp, the lethality rung, the readouts and execution's playback all inherit with no second copy of anything, and the magazine round it spends is `AttackAction.execute`'s existing readiness clause with nothing added.

**Counter-immunity is STRUCTURAL, not a filter.** The shots live in `ResolvedPlan.watch_shots`, and `calculate_reactions_for_squad` reads `plan.attacks` — so "a derived attack is never counter-bait" is true because the list it would have to be in is a different list, rather than because somebody remembered to skip them.

**The footprint freezes at EXECUTE, not at declare.** `OverwatchAction` stamps the attack and the aim (the `fired_attack` pattern, Law #2 provenance) and derives its cells from the actor's *projected* cell every resolve, exactly as a stored attack aim re-targets — so re-planning the walk that precedes the declaration moves the preview honestly. The geometry becomes a fact on the `Watch` when the order executes and the watcher has arrived. `OverwatchAction.watched_cells_from(origin)` is the one spelling both callers use.

**Declaration rides the normal targeting flow with TWO forking lines** (one until #591). `game.aim_intent` says whether the click queues a shot or a watch, and — since #591 — what the aim is painted in; reach geometry, facing and the legality check are the same code. A second `GameState` would have been a second copy of the whole aiming handler for one branch. The paint fork is one call, `overlay_manager.set_aim_colors(aiming, intent == AimIntent.WATCH)`, and `OverlayManager._aiming_watch` is THE store for it on that side: written only there, read by both layers' colour *and* by the tile pulse's two endpoints, so the three cannot disagree. **That pulse is the trap the fix had to clear** — `Pulse` holds the endpoints it was STARTED with, so endpoints read off the constants breathe a watch aim back to the shot's yellow twice a second, and a fix on the mode-entry path alone looks right in a screenshot and is wrong in motion. `exit_current_mode` resets the fork beside `aim_intent` for a second reason: the HOVER layer is shared with `PICKING_TARGET`, so a watch tint left standing would colour the next rescue's tile pick.

Rulings the doc did not spell out, made at build time and open to argument:

- **Overwatch is a WEAPON ACTION and ONE ROW** (dev, 2026-08-26: *"Overwatch should be a weapon action, not it's own top rung on the menu"*, then *"The Carbine will get 1 Overwatch action as a special weapon action. That's it. There will be no sub menu."* -- both correcting the build's first answer, which gave it a top-level ring of its own that expanded into per-attack children). It sits in the kit slice beside Reload and clicking it picks the attack. That also moves the GATE onto `Unit.has_weapon_actions()`: a watch the unit could not fire right now no longer opens the slice by itself, exactly as an uncharged Blowback does not, and it still LISTS greyed with its attack's own reason once something else opens the slice. **Each watch row is named by ITS ATTACK, with nothing synthesised** — which is only safe because a watch attack is watch-ONLY (below), so it has no firing row to collide with, and the Carbine's own watch is a `.tres` literally called *Overwatch*. The row used to fork on *is this the weapon's default attack* and hand anything else its `display_name` — the same name its own fire row already wore — so [#590](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/590) ate the watch row in `MainActionMenu.build_tree`'s duplicate-name guard and left the weapon main's watch as the only reachable one.
- **`can_overwatch` makes an attack an overwatch attack and ONLY that** (dev, 2026-08-27: *"when I check the 'can overwatch' box, I expect to get an attack that is an overwatch, and only an overwatch. It isn't an either or thing."*). It is not a capability sitting alongside firing: a watch attack is declared as a standing watch, never fired directly, so it appears in no attack menu, the AI never picks it, and it cannot be a weapon's main — `WeaponTemplateLint` refuses that outright, since a watch-only main leaves the weapon nothing to attack with. `EquippableData` splits whatever a source carries into two DISJOINT views, `fireable_only` and `watch_attacks`, off one `repertoire()`; every fire surface runs through the first and `Unit.overwatch_attacks` reads the second. Composition is unchanged: `WeaponModData.can_overwatch_override` grants or revokes exactly like `hits_allies_override`, OFF beating ON, so fitting order can never change what a weapon can do — an ON now MOVES an attack from the fire view to the watch view rather than adding a verb to it.
- **The Carbine carries a separate watch attack, not a dual-purpose main** (2026-08-27). `Resources/WeaponAttacks/Overwatch.tres` is a stock `extra_attacks` entry on the family — the main's geometry and numbers exactly (Manhattan exactly 2, and the same `requires_readiness`/`consumes_readiness`, so a dry carbine still cannot stand watch) — and `Shot` is a plain fireable main again. It was authored ON `Shot` at #413 build time, which the rule above made impossible. Nothing about the watch changed: one cell at standoff 2, metered by the magazine, exactly as [weapons.md](weapons.md) predicted; Watchman's Sear's lane ([weapon-mod-ideas.md](weapon-mod-ideas.md) #26) is untouched, and a granted line watch is still the mod content it always was.
- **A halted walk defers to a shove.** `Unit.projected_cell`'s "a real move beats the shove: you walked out from under it" gains "...unless the pass stopped you", because a crosser the shot threw did not walk out from under anything.
- **The watch is spent by the SHOT, above every early-out** — a triggered shot that whiffs or lands on an empty cell has still been taken.
- **`OVERWATCH` sits immediately before `GUARD` in `SIDE_CHANNEL_ORDER`**, for GUARD's own reason: a watch armed this pass must arm after every hit it was resolved against has played back, or a shove combo that already spent it would hand execution a live watch.

**A TRIGGERED SHOT PLAYS AT THE MOMENT IT WAS TRIGGERED — [#567](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/567), BUILT 2026-08-28.** #413 shipped a declared v1 cut: every shot played in one batch *after* the move phase, so the carbine fired at a cell the crosser had already left, and (unnoticed until #567 grepped `fire_watch_entries` and found **two** call sites) a shot a SHOVE set off played *before the blow that threw them into it*. The dev took both halves in one trip — one rule rather than one fix and one heir.

- **A walk HALTS at the crossing cell, the shot plays, the walk resumes** — and only that walk halts, so "units are still shown moving together" survives with a beat in it. `MoveAction.execute` walks one leg per stretch between interrupts and parks between them; `OrderExecutor._execute_move_phase` is the poll that plays the shot and releases it.
- **The shots play in RESOLVE order, never in arrival order.** Only the HEAD of the pending list is ever looked at, so a crosser that reaches its cell first stands there until its turn — #412's whole payoff is that dragging a move row changes who eats the shot, and a playback sorted by wall clock would show the opposite.
- **A shove-triggered shot plays after the volley that threw them**, spliced into the attack phase by `ResolvedPlan.attack_playback()` — a playback list, never written back to `plan.attacks`, because a triggered shot inside that list would be counter-bait (the structural rule above).
- **The resolve records the MOMENT, playback decides the PAUSE.** `AttackAction.triggered_during` / `triggered_at_step` say which order was playing and how far into it; `MoveAction.resolved_stop_index` stays the one authority on where a walk ENDS. Nothing about when damage lands changed — this moved pixels only.
- **A triggered shot is a BEAT now.** It sits on the `BeatSheet` like any volley, which retired two hand-built schedules in `OrderExecutor` and gave the shot its rung-aware hold, its linger and #520's push-in for free. Its camera subject is still the CROSSER (the rule moved into `BeatSheet.Beat.subject()`), and the walk's framing is returned to after each interrupt (dev, 2026-08-28).
- **A shove-triggered shot's cells are STAGED** (`BeatSheet._gather_cells` reads the playback list): it now fires inside the tear-out diorama, and the watcher's anchor cell is nobody's attacker origin. Mid-walk shots are deliberately not staged — they play on the board, before the tear-out.

**The aim-time read was owed until #591 and is now built** (see the pre-aimed-shot clause above), along with the queued state. What the mark itself is made of is a separate question, answered below.

**The mark's ART — settled #591, 2026-08-27.** It shipped with #413 as PLACEHOLDER, and that placeholder was worse than a stand-in rather than merely unfinished: it was literally `CursorIcon.png`, the same file `CursorState.DEFAULT` draws, so a footprint tiled into plus-shapes at the shared corners and the cursor standing on a watched cell was invisible inside them. Neither art pack in the repo has a crosshair (ProjectUtumno is monsters and items, the other is architecture), so candidates were **drawn** rather than cut — the *the dev picks* rule the Guard shield followed, one step earlier in the pipeline. **He took the RETICLE** (`Art/Icons/BoardIcons/WatchMarkIcon.png`) — a broken circle with four inward ticks and a centre dot, the most literal *something is aimed at you* — over a diamond, a boxed cross and a flash-burst: white ink plus a 1px black halo, `SquadRingIcon`'s trick, so black survives the hue modulate and one texture serves whatever `WATCH_MARK_COLOR` is tuned to. **The QUEUE ROW wears it too** (`OverwatchAction.OVERWATCH_ICON` now IS `WATCH_MARK_TEXTURE`) — one texture, one answer to what a watch looks like, exactly as Guard's row wears the ward shield.

Two rules the pick paid for. Authored at ONE cell (16px), because a 32px cut is a four-cell decal — how #414's ward mark first shipped, reading as *the marker isn't showing up*. And the one worth carrying if this is ever re-cut: **candidates must be rendered on a real contiguous FOOTPRINT, never on one cell, because a mark presenting a straight run perpendicular to the cell edge lines up with its neighbour's and the pair reads as one bar bridging them** — three of the first six died on exactly that, reading as a net rather than as per-cell threat marks. The reticle's ticks do this mildly and were **accepted knowing it** (he compared it against the diamond, which does not, and took the reticle anyway); shortening them a pixel is the fix if it reads badly in play.

## Guard ([#414](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/414)) — **BUILT 2026-08-21**

**A main action: pick a unit in range and become its bodyguard — the next hit that would land on them lands on you instead.** Named **Guard**, not "Defend," to stay distinct from DEF the stat (Glossary entries at build time keep the two legible side by side); it **absorbs** [jobs.md](jobs.md)'s unbuilt "Guard redirects" follow-up — one mechanic, many sources, rather than a jobs-side guard and a weapon-side defend answering the same question twice (Law #4).

- **Access — the working model (a lean, playtest-subject; the one deliberately-unresolved fork on Guard):** Guard is a **basic main action everyone has**. What kit grants — weapons (the floated Aegis-class drill), jobs, armor, carvings, source-agnostic — is the **brace bonus**: bonus DEF applied to the absorbed instance; bare Guard substitutes at +0. Every other ruling in this doc is gate-independent, which is what lets this fork stay open through playtest without destabilizing anything. Implementation note for whenever it's picked up: the ability union (`add_live`, innate→jobs→gear) is the only existing multi-source "does the kit grant X" seam — the incumbent, per Law #4; weapons-as-ability-source is the one blocked decision it touches, and it opens when the first weapon-granted bonus ships, not before.
- **Total victim substitution.** The blocked hit resolves against the blocker as if they were the target — the blocker's DEF and armor **plus the brace bonus**, and the *entire* payload: damage, elemental states, knockback, all of it, resolved from the blocker's own cell. The ward takes nothing, ever. It's a per-hit hook in the resolver (Iron Will's shape — instantaneous within one resolution, not a `StatEffect`), so every pipeline stage just runs with a different victim and the preview shows the blocker's real projected HP and lethality rung. Why substitution: it's what carves the tank role — bodyguarding with your armored unit *works* because the tank's mitigation applies; bodyguarding with a second medic is a sacrifice. Bleed-through-to-the-ward was considered and **dropped, not deferred** — the brace bonus is the one block-quality knob.
- **The pair is unit-and-range, not cells** (dev correction, superseding the scratchpad entry's own "must stay in the cell" disarm): the Guard targets a specific **unit** and holds while that unit is **within the Guard's range** of the blocker — range authored on the granting content, default 1. An AoE wind blast shoving both back a cell together keeps the ward; separation beyond range drops it, however it happens. Range is checked at each hit's resolution moment, so mid-pass displacements between sequential hits move the answer, previewed. (The rejected literal reading — "protect whoever stands on the square" — would let an enemy shoved onto the vacated cell inherit your tank; unit-and-range kills it for free.)
- **Catches ANY damaging hit on the ward** — authored attack, counter, overwatch shot, hostile or friendly — except an already-substituted hit (chains on hold, above). Heals and `deals_no_damage` utility pass through (nobody wants the tank intercepting the medic's heal). Friendly-fire coverage is player agency, not a foot-gun: the Guard arms at its queue slot, so sequence your splashy attack before it.
- **"Can't defend while moving" (dev ruling, blessed as structural design):** no Guard is ever live during its own faction's move phase — last turn's lapsed at turn start, this turn's arms after moves. So a mover crossing a watch is never Guard-covered: **Overwatch owns punishing voluntary movement, Guard owns the stationary window**, and the overlap exists exactly where the enemy manufactures it — an enemy shoving your ward through a line on *their* phase fires a shot your live Guard CAN block.
- **AoE ruling — one blast, one moment.** Within a single attack's volley, standing-reaction liveness (range + lifecycle) reads **at the volley's start**; the damage packets then thread HP as usual. A blast covering blocker and ward double-bills the blocker — his own share at normal mitigation plus the ward's at the brace bonus — and if the sum downs him, **he goes down having blocked**. Chosen over sequential-sibling threading because that would let the medic's life hinge on volley *gather order* — an order the player neither authors nor sees, the exact opposite of #412's order-is-a-player-lever doctrine. This narrows [resolution-pipeline.md](resolution-pipeline.md)'s deferred volley fork for reaction liveness only; tile-state threading within a volley stays deferred.
- **Stacked Guards are legal** — two blockers may ward the same unit; the earlier-queued Guard absorbs the first hit. Queue-order vocabulary settling precedence, no refusal rule.
- **The counterplay set, all authored, all previewed:** a pierce flag on the attack (`AttackData`, the scratchpad's own idea); shove the *blocker* out of range (note a knockback hit on a Guard shoves the blocker — the disarm is a combo target); shove the *ward*; or **AoE the cluster** and double-bill the tank. Bodyguarding forces clustering; area damage is its natural predator.
- **Presentation:** the ward link rides the **ground marker channel** ([#346](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/346)'s rule — ground = the interaction): a shield icon under the defendee with a connecting mark from the defender, one clear mark under the restraint doctrine, both views or declared on #292. **BUILT as specified ([#450](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/450), 2026-08-26)** — the shield is the defendee's alone, and the connector is the project's own path-arrow trail run one step from the defender (dev call: *the same arrows we use for everything else*), tinted by `OverlayManager.GUARD_LINK_MODULATE`, which ships neutral white so the Game tab's picker is the whole colour. **Its head stops HALF A CELL SHORT, on the edge the pair shares** (round 2, dev in play: *"the shield just isn't as readable as I'd like it to be under the arrowhead"*) — measured, not eyeballed: the arrowhead tile's ink runs x=0–11 of 16 and the shield's x=1–13, so drawn on the ward's own cell the arrow covered eleven of the shield's thirteen columns. Only the head moves, so the link still visibly crosses; the inset is a Game-tab knob (`GUARD_LINK_HEAD_INSET`, ~0.7 clears the shield outright) whose sweep is the game's own redraw rather than a re-tint, because it MOVES a sprite. A sub-cell position is honest in both views — `OverlayMirror._anchor_px` keeps true pixels through `BoardSpace.of_pixels` — which is the exact opposite of `scale` and `offset`, neither of which `_marker` carries. The arrow atlas has no tile for a diagonal or a gap, so the link is drawn only across one orthogonal step; `GUARD_BASE_RANGE` is 1, and a pair a shove has dragged apart keeps its shield and loses its arrow. **The queue row wears the same shield as the board** — one texture, one answer to what Guard looks like. **A QUEUED Guard draws that same pair GHOSTED**, at the alpha the board already means *planned, not yet real* by (the pending-ignite preview's), because every other queued verb previews and drawing a plan identically to live cover would tell the player they are protected before they are; `GuardWard.sequence` already separates the two (0 = made, ≥1 = armed), so nothing new stores it. The **slice-one block moment reuses the existing attack lunge** — defender lunges toward defendee at the block; the *visually loud* jump-in-front the scratchpad wanted is saved for battle-zoom animations (noted on [#358](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/358)'s sprite-FX stack — no bespoke animation machinery ahead of it). **That justification is half-spent as of [#629](https://github.com/Phaazoid/Godoiosis/issues/629) (2026-08-28): the machinery now EXISTS — a unit sprite can play frames — so what the block moment still waits on is the authored GESTURE and the beat that fires it (#603), not the ability to play one. The rule is unchanged; only the reason it is deferred has moved.**

### How it was built (#414, 2026-08-21) — and the rulings the build had to make

**Substitution is a VICTIM REWRITE inside the resolve pass, not a new stage.** `SquadManager.resolve_plan` already expands every stored aim into *derived* `AttackAction`s each pass (the #15 rule), so the resolver rewrites the derived hit's `target` to the blocker and stamps `AttackAction.blocked_for` with who it was aimed at. That is this doc's own sentence — *"every pipeline stage just runs with a different victim"* — made literal: DEF mitigation, elemental immunity, Iron Will, the knockback direction and landing, the elevation stamp, the lethality rung, execution's playback, the headless twin and the queue row all inherit the substitution with no second copy of anything. The player's stored aim is untouched.

**Liveness is ONE query fed by two projections of the same fact** (`ResolvedPlan.guards`, in arm order): wards armed in an EARLIER pass, copied off the units oldest-first, plus Guards queued in THIS plan, appended by `resolve_plan`'s own queue walk when it reaches their slot. That append point *is* "arms at its queue slot" — an attack expanded earlier simply never sees the entry, with no index bookkeeping and no second authority. The relationship is `MoveAction` ↔ `movement.cell` through `get_projected_destination()`, not a duplicate seam. The pass mutates COPIES; the live ward is spent by execution.

**A Guard that ate its owner's own splash arms ALREADY SPENT.** Side-channel verbs execute after the attack phase, so without this the queue would preview a used Guard and execution would hand back a live one. The resolver stamps `GuardAction.resolved_spent` each pass (the R8 "write the outcome onto the action" pattern, for an order with no `ResolvedOutcome` of its own) and the queue row says so.

Three rulings the doc did not spell out, decided at build time and open to argument:

- **The blocker's party counters, not the ward's.** `calculate_reactions_for_squad` reads the hit's victim, which is now the blocker — total substitution applied honestly. It only differs from the old behaviour when blocker and ward are in different squads.
- **A Guard never shields anyone from its own blocker's swing.** Only reachable through an AoE counter (one main action per turn means a unit can never both Guard and attack), and absorbing your own attack on someone else's behalf is incoherent rather than funny.
- **Wards are allies, alive, self excluded; range is manhattan distance with no line-of-sight and no height gate** — Guard is a stance, not an attack. A DOWNED ally stays a legal ward on purpose: a body waiting on a rescue is exactly what someone might stand over.

Shipped beside the mechanic: `AttackData.pierces_guard` (the authored counterplay flag), `Abilities.Id.BRACE` + `BRACE_DEF_BONUS` riding the ability union and authored onto **Bulwark Plate**, `GUARD_BASE_RANGE`, a ground-channel ward marker (real shields as of 2026-08-21, cut from `ProjectUtumno_full` row 38 — the dev's picks; #450 then made it **shield-plus-link** as the design always asked, so col 35 is the one surviving cut and col 30 was deleted with the row's second shield), and the live ward in the save snapshot as an entry-index re-link. **The access fork stays open**: Guard ships basic-for-everyone, and every ruling above is gate-independent, so playtest can still flip it.
